# 16.4 Cap Table Design for DAOs

DAOs do not have traditional capitalization tables in the equity sense -- there are no share classes, preferred stock, or option pools. A DAO typically has a single governance token class where every token carries identical rights. Yet the cap table concept remains critically relevant because different stakeholders acquired tokens at different prices, with different vesting schedules, lock-up periods, and liquidity profiles. Understanding who owns what, at what cost basis, and when they can sell is essential for the investment banker advising on capital raises, M&A transactions, and treasury management.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Explain why cap table analysis matters for a single-token-class DAO
- Identify the five components of a DAO cap table
- Use on-chain and off-chain tools for cap table tracking
- Calculate pre-money and post-money dilution from a capital raise
- Model cap table evolution over a 3-5 year horizon
{% endhint %}

## 16.4.1 The DAO Cap Table Concept

### Why Cap Tables Matter for DAOs

Despite the single token class, DAO cap table analysis serves several critical functions:

| Function | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Dilution analysis** | Quantifying the ownership impact of new token issuances on existing holders |
| **Governance power mapping** | Understanding voting power concentration and dynamics |
| **Investor relations** | Tracking institutional positions, cost bases, and unlock schedules |
| **Capital raise pricing** | Benchmarking new sale terms against prior rounds |
| **Sell pressure forecasting** | Predicting future selling based on unlock schedules and cost bases |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**The On-Chain Transparency Advantage**

Unlike traditional cap tables (maintained privately by company counsel), DAO cap tables are largely reconstructible from on-chain data. Token transfers, vesting contracts, and governance delegation are all publicly visible. This transparency is both an advantage (comprehensive data) and a challenge (requires sophisticated analytics to synthesize).
{% endhint %}

### Cap Table vs. Token Distribution

The cap table goes beyond a simple snapshot of current token balances. It incorporates historical context and forward-looking dynamics:

| Dimension | Token Distribution (Snapshot) | Cap Table (Full Analysis) |
|-----------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| **What it shows** | Current token balances by address | Ownership by stakeholder class with full context |
| **Cost basis** | Not captured | Acquisition price per token for each cohort |
| **Vesting status** | Not captured | Locked, vesting, and fully unlocked positions |
| **Governance power** | Raw balance only | Delegated voting power and influence mapping |
| **Future dynamics** | Static | Projects unlock schedules, dilution, burns |

## 16.4.2 Cap Table Components

A comprehensive DAO cap table breaks ownership into five stakeholder categories, each with distinct characteristics.

### 1. Foundation / Team Holdings

Tokens allocated to the founding team, core contributors, and the protocol foundation or development company.

| Attribute | Typical Profile |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Allocation** | 15-25% of total supply |
| **Vesting** | 3-4 year vesting with 1-year cliff |
| **Cost basis** | Near-zero (allocated, not purchased) |
| **Governance role** | Often significant voting power; may self-delegate or abstain |
| **Sell pressure risk** | High post-cliff; concentrated positions |

### 2. Investor Holdings

Tokens purchased by venture capital firms, funds, and institutional investors across seed, private, and OTC rounds.

| Attribute | Typical Profile |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Allocation** | 10-20% of total supply across rounds |
| **Vesting** | Round-specific; typically 1-2 year lock + 1-2 year vest |
| **Cost basis** | Varies by round (seed lowest, OTC highest) |
| **Governance role** | Active delegation or direct voting; often Tier 1 delegates |
| **Sell pressure risk** | Moderate; fund lifecycles may force exits |

### 3. Community Holdings

Tokens distributed to the community through airdrops, liquidity mining, contributor grants, bounties, and open market purchases.

| Attribute | Typical Profile |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Allocation** | 30-50% of total supply |
| **Vesting** | Generally fully liquid (airdrops) or short vesting (grants) |
| **Cost basis** | Highly variable (free for airdrop, market price for buyers) |
| **Governance role** | Diffuse; most holders do not actively govern |
| **Sell pressure risk** | Highest for airdrop recipients; lowest for long-term holders |

### 4. Treasury Holdings

Tokens retained in the DAO treasury for future use -- capital raises, contributor compensation, ecosystem grants, and strategic initiatives.

| Attribute | Typical Profile |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Allocation** | 20-40% of total supply at TGE; decreases over time |
| **Vesting** | Controlled by governance; no automatic unlock |
| **Cost basis** | N/A (DAO's own tokens) |
| **Governance role** | Typically non-voting to prevent self-dealing |
| **Sell pressure risk** | Governance-controlled; represents future dilution potential |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Treasury Non-Voting Convention**

Most well-governed DAOs exclude treasury tokens from voting power calculations. If the DAO could vote its own tokens, governance would be self-referential -- the DAO could approve proposals to benefit its own treasury without genuine stakeholder consensus. Some protocols enforce this at the smart contract level; others rely on convention.
{% endhint %}

### 5. Circulating Supply Analysis

Understanding the relationship between total supply, circulating supply, and liquid supply.

```
Total Supply:        All tokens that exist (minted)
Circulating Supply:  Total supply - Treasury - Locked/Vesting tokens
Liquid Supply:       Circulating supply actually available for trading
Float:               Liquid supply / Total supply
```

| Metric | Formula | Significance |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Circulating Supply** | Total - Treasury - Locked | Relevant for market cap calculations |
| **Float** | Liquid Supply / Total Supply | Low float = higher price impact from trades |
| **Insider Ownership** | (Team + Investors) / Total | Concentration risk indicator |
| **Community Ownership** | Community / Total | Decentralization metric |

## 16.4.3 Cap Table Management Tools

The investment banker uses a combination of on-chain analytics and governance platforms to maintain and analyze the DAO cap table.

### On-Chain Analytics

| Tool | Primary Function | Cap Table Use |
|------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Dune Analytics** | Custom SQL queries on blockchain data | Build custom cap table dashboards; track token flows by cohort |
| **Nansen** | Labeled address analytics | Identify institutional wallets; track smart money flows |
| **Arkham** | Entity-level blockchain intelligence | Map related addresses to single entities; identify investor positions |
| **Etherscan / Block Explorers** | Transaction-level data | Verify specific transfers; audit vesting contract states |

### Vesting and Payment Tracking

| Tool | Function | Cap Table Use |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Sablier** | Token streaming and vesting | Track real-time vesting status for team and investors |
| **Superfluid** | Continuous payment streams | Monitor ongoing contributor compensation flows |
| **LlamaPay** | Multi-chain streaming payments | Track cross-chain vesting and payment schedules |

### Governance and Delegation

| Tool | Function | Cap Table Use |
|------|----------|---------------|
| **Tally** | On-chain governance analytics | Map voting power distribution and delegation patterns |
| **Boardroom** | Cross-protocol governance data | Compare governance concentration across protocols |
| **Snapshot** | Off-chain voting | Track off-chain delegation and proposal participation |

{% hint style="success" %}
**Building the Cap Table Dashboard**

The most effective approach is a **Dune Analytics dashboard** that combines:

1. **Token distribution pie chart** -- Team, Investors, Community, Treasury percentages
2. **Vesting schedule timeline** -- Upcoming unlocks by cohort (bar chart, monthly)
3. **Top holder table** -- Largest addresses with labels, balances, and % of supply
4. **Governance power heatmap** -- Voting power concentration by delegate
5. **Historical flows** -- Net accumulation/distribution by cohort over time

This dashboard should be updated automatically and linked in the quarterly governance report.
{% endhint %}

## 16.4.4 Dilution Analysis

Dilution analysis quantifies the ownership impact of issuing new tokens. Every token sale, contributor grant, and incentive emission dilutes existing holders.

### Dilution Formulas

```
Pre-Money Ownership:
  Pre-money % = Holder's Tokens / Pre-Raise Total Supply

Post-Money Ownership:
  Post-money % = Holder's Tokens / (Pre-Raise Supply + New Tokens Issued)

Dilution (absolute):
  Dilution = Pre-money % - Post-money %

Dilution (relative):
  Relative Dilution % = (Pre-money % - Post-money %) / Pre-money % x 100
```

### Worked Example

{% hint style="info" %}
**Scenario**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Pre-raise total supply | 1,000,000,000 tokens |
| New tokens sold (OTC) | 50,000,000 tokens (5% of pre-raise supply) |
| Holder's position | 10,000,000 tokens |
{% endhint %}

```
Pre-Money Ownership:
  10,000,000 / 1,000,000,000 = 1.000%

Post-Money Ownership:
  10,000,000 / (1,000,000,000 + 50,000,000)
  10,000,000 / 1,050,000,000 = 0.952%

Absolute Dilution:
  1.000% - 0.952% = 0.048 percentage points

Relative Dilution:
  (1.000% - 0.952%) / 1.000% x 100 = 4.76%
```

### Framing Dilution for Governance

{% hint style="success" %}
**Dilution Communication Framework**

When presenting a capital raise to governance, frame dilution in terms of value exchange:

> "This raise dilutes all token holders by approximately 4.8%. In exchange, the DAO receives $X million in stablecoins, extending the operating runway by Y months and funding [specific strategic initiative]."

Always pair dilution with the **concrete benefit** the capital provides. Governance is far more likely to approve a raise when they understand the value trade-off, not just the cost.
{% endhint %}

### Anti-Dilution in DAOs

Unlike traditional venture capital, where preferred shareholders often have anti-dilution protection (ratchets, weighted average adjustments), DAOs typically do not provide anti-dilution provisions. All token holders are diluted equally by new issuances.

However, sophisticated institutional investors may negotiate side agreements:

| Mechanism | Description | DAO Considerations |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Pro-rata rights** | Right to participate in future rounds to maintain ownership % | Reasonable; aligns incentives for follow-on |
| **Price protection** | Discount adjustment if subsequent round occurs at lower price | Risky; creates complex obligations |
| **Token grants** | Additional tokens issued if dilution exceeds threshold | Unusual; creates circular dilution risk |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Avoid Price Protection Clauses**

Price protection (anti-dilution ratchets) creates dangerous dynamics in DAO governance. If the DAO raises a subsequent round at a lower valuation, triggering additional token issuance to the protected investor, it creates a reflexive spiral: more tokens issued lowers the per-token value, potentially triggering further adjustments. The investment banker should strongly advise against price protection provisions.
{% endhint %}

## 16.4.5 Modeling the Cap Table Over Time

A static cap table snapshot is useful but insufficient. The investment banker should build a **dynamic 3-5 year model** that projects cap table evolution incorporating all known and planned token flows.

### Model Inputs

| Input Category | Specific Items |
|---------------|---------------|
| **Team vesting** | Cliff dates, vesting schedules, and expected forfeitures |
| **Investor vesting** | Round-specific lock-up expirations and vesting cadence |
| **Community distributions** | Planned airdrops, incentive programs, and grant budgets |
| **Treasury disbursements** | Budgeted contributor compensation, ecosystem grants, operational spending |
| **New capital raises** | Planned OTC sales, bond issuances, or convertible note conversions |
| **Token burns** | Fee burns, buyback programs, or deflationary mechanics |
| **Inflation / Emissions** | Scheduled token emissions (if inflationary model) |

### Model Structure

```
For each period (quarterly, 3-5 years):

  Beginning Supply
  + New Emissions (inflation schedule)
  + Convertible Note Conversions (if triggered)
  - Token Burns (fee burns, buybacks)
  = End-of-Period Total Supply

  By Stakeholder:
    Team:       Beginning + Newly Vested - Sold/Transferred
    Investors:  Beginning + Newly Unlocked - Sold/Transferred
    Community:  Beginning + Airdrops + Mining Rewards + Purchases - Sales
    Treasury:   Beginning - Grants - Compensation - OTC Sales - Burns
    Circulating: Total - Treasury - Locked
```

### Example: 3-Year Cap Table Projection

| Component | Year 0 (Current) | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|-----------|-----------------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Total Supply** | 1,000M | 1,020M | 1,040M | 1,050M |
| **Team (locked)** | 150M | 100M | 50M | 0M |
| **Team (unlocked)** | 50M | 100M | 150M | 200M |
| **Investors (locked)** | 100M | 60M | 20M | 0M |
| **Investors (unlocked)** | 50M | 90M | 130M | 150M |
| **Community** | 300M | 340M | 370M | 390M |
| **Treasury** | 350M | 310M | 280M | 260M |
| **Circulating Supply** | 400M | 530M | 650M | 740M |
| **Float %** | 40.0% | 52.0% | 62.5% | 70.5% |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Key Insights from Dynamic Modeling**

The model above reveals several important dynamics:

1. **Float increases significantly** (40% to 70.5%) as vesting completes -- expect increased sell pressure
2. **Treasury decreases** (350M to 260M) from operational spending -- monitor runway
3. **Team unlock cliff** (Year 1-2) creates potential concentrated selling periods
4. **Community share grows** (30% to 37%) indicating healthy decentralization trend

These projections inform capital raise timing -- raising capital before major unlock events avoids competing with insider selling for market absorption.
{% endhint %}

### Scenario Analysis

The dynamic model should include multiple scenarios to stress-test the cap table:

| Scenario | Assumptions | Impact |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| **Base case** | Planned raises, normal burn, scheduled emissions | Reference projection |
| **Bull case** | Accelerated burns, token appreciation reduces need for new raises | Lower dilution, higher float value |
| **Bear case** | Additional emergency raises, reduced burns, extended contributor vesting | Higher dilution, faster treasury depletion |
| **M&A scenario** | Token swap or acquisition adding new token holders | Significant cap table restructuring |

```
Key Metrics to Track Across Scenarios:

  Treasury Runway (months) = Stablecoin Treasury / Monthly Burn Rate
  Dilution per Year (%) = New Tokens / Beginning Supply
  Float Growth Rate = Change in Circulating / Change in Total Supply
  Insider Ownership Decline = (Team + Investor %) Year 0 vs. Year N
```

{% hint style="success" %}
**Presenting the Cap Table Model to Governance**

The dynamic cap table model should be presented to governance alongside any capital raise proposal. Include:

1. **Current state** -- who owns what today
2. **Post-raise state** -- immediate dilution from the proposed raise
3. **3-year projection** -- how the cap table evolves with the raise vs. without
4. **Scenario analysis** -- dilution under bull, base, and bear market conditions
5. **Key trade-offs** -- dilution cost vs. capital benefit, clearly quantified

This comprehensive presentation enables informed governance decision-making and demonstrates the investment banker's analytical rigor.
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- DAO cap tables matter despite single token classes -- different stakeholders have different cost bases, vesting schedules, and liquidity profiles that drive governance power dynamics and sell pressure

- The five cap table components (team, investors, community, treasury, circulating supply) each have distinct characteristics that the investment banker must track and analyze

- On-chain analytics tools (Dune, Nansen, Arkham) combined with governance platforms (Tally, Boardroom) enable comprehensive cap table reconstruction from public data

- Dilution analysis should always be framed as a value exchange -- quantify both the dilution cost and the capital benefit to governance

- Anti-dilution ratchets and price protection clauses should be avoided in DAO capital raises due to reflexive spiral risk

- Dynamic 3-5 year cap table models with scenario analysis are essential for capital raise planning, timing investor lock-up expirations, and forecasting governance power shifts
