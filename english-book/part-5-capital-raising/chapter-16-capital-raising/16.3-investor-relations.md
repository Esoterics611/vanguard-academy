# 16.3 Investor Relations for DAOs

Investor relations (IR) in a DAO context is fundamentally broader than its traditional counterpart. While a public company's IR function communicates primarily with equity analysts and institutional shareholders, a DAO's IR function must engage a diverse constituency: token holders, governance delegates, institutional investors, ecosystem partners, contributors, and the broader community. The investment banker advising a DAO must help build this function from the ground up, establishing the reporting standards, communication cadence, and stakeholder engagement practices that institutional capital expects.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Define the DAO IR function and its broader constituency compared to traditional IR
- Identify the five core activities of DAO investor relations
- Build a quarterly governance report covering financial, operational, governance, and strategic metrics
- Design a delegate relations strategy with mapping, engagement, and accountability components
- Implement crisis communication protocols for adverse events
{% endhint %}

## 16.3.1 The DAO IR Function

### Broader Constituency

Traditional public company IR focuses on a narrow audience: institutional shareholders, sell-side analysts, and rating agencies. DAO IR must serve a much wider set of stakeholders, each with different information needs and communication preferences.

| Stakeholder | Information Needs | Communication Channel |
|-------------|------------------|----------------------|
| **Token holders** | Financial performance, token value drivers, dilution events | Forum posts, governance calls, dashboards |
| **Governance delegates** | Proposal context, strategic rationale, vote implications | Direct outreach, delegate calls, briefing docs |
| **Institutional investors** | Financial statements, KPIs, risk factors, strategic outlook | Quarterly reports, investor calls, 1:1 meetings |
| **Ecosystem partners** | Integration roadmap, partnership opportunities, technical updates | Partner channels, BD meetings |
| **Contributors** | Compensation, runway, org health, strategic priorities | Internal comms, town halls |
| **Community (users)** | Product updates, security, governance participation | Discord, Twitter/X, forum, blog |

### Core IR Activities

The DAO IR function encompasses five core activities:

#### 1. Regular Financial Reporting

Producing standardized financial reports on a consistent schedule -- monthly summaries, quarterly deep dives, and annual reviews. Reports should follow the financial statement frameworks from Chapter 2.

{% hint style="success" %}
**Reporting Cadence**

| Report | Frequency | Audience | Depth |
|--------|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Treasury Dashboard** | Real-time | All stakeholders | Balances, allocations, runway |
| **Monthly Summary** | Monthly | Community, token holders | Key metrics, notable events |
| **Quarterly Governance Report** | Quarterly | Institutional investors, delegates | Comprehensive (see 16.3.2) |
| **Annual Review** | Annually | All stakeholders | Year-in-review, forward strategy |

Consistency is more important than perfection. A DAO that publishes a reliable monthly update builds more credibility than one that produces sporadic but detailed reports.
{% endhint %}

#### 2. Governance Communication

Providing context and analysis for governance proposals -- especially those with financial implications. Token holders and delegates need clear explanations of what is being proposed, why it matters, and what the trade-offs are.

#### 3. Strategic Updates

Communicating the DAO's strategic direction, competitive positioning, and key milestones. These updates help investors and delegates evaluate whether the DAO is executing against its stated priorities.

#### 4. Investor Engagement

Proactive outreach to existing and prospective institutional investors -- 1:1 meetings, investor calls, conference presentations, and relationship maintenance. This is the closest analog to traditional IR.

#### 5. Crisis Communication

Rapid, transparent communication during adverse events -- exploits, governance attacks, regulatory actions, or market dislocations. The speed and quality of crisis communication directly impacts the DAO's credibility and recovery trajectory.

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Crisis Communication Protocol**

When an adverse event occurs:

1. **Acknowledge immediately** (within 1 hour) -- confirm awareness, state what is known
2. **Provide regular updates** (every 2-4 hours) -- new information, actions taken, expected timeline
3. **Deliver root cause analysis** (within 1-2 weeks) -- what happened, why, what changes will prevent recurrence
4. **Follow up on remediation** (ongoing) -- status updates on corrective actions

**Never** minimize the severity, speculate without evidence, or go silent during a crisis. Transparency builds trust; silence destroys it.
{% endhint %}

## 16.3.2 The Quarterly Governance Report

The quarterly governance report is the flagship IR document -- the DAO equivalent of a public company's 10-Q filing. It provides institutional-grade reporting across four sections: financial, operational, governance, and strategic outlook.

### Section 1: Financial Summary

| Metric | Current Quarter | Prior Quarter | YoY Change |
|--------|----------------|---------------|------------|
| **Protocol Revenue** | $ | $ | % |
| **Operating Expenses** | $ | $ | % |
| **Net Income** | $ | $ | % |
| **Treasury Balance (Total)** | $ | $ | % |
| **Treasury Balance (Stablecoins)** | $ | $ | % |
| **Runway (months)** | # | # | +/- |

**Additional financial disclosures:**
- Revenue breakdown by source (trading fees, interest, liquidations, etc.)
- Expense breakdown by category (contributors, grants, infrastructure, legal)
- Treasury composition (pie chart: stablecoins, ETH, native token, other)
- Any capital raises or disbursements during the quarter

### Section 2: Operational Summary

| Metric | Current Quarter | Prior Quarter | QoQ Change |
|--------|----------------|---------------|------------|
| **Total Value Locked (TVL)** | $ | $ | % |
| **Trading Volume** | $ | $ | % |
| **Active Users (monthly)** | # | # | % |
| **Transactions** | # | # | % |

**Additional operational disclosures:**
- Key milestones achieved (product launches, chain deployments, partnerships)
- Security posture (audits completed, bug bounty activity, incidents)
- Contributor headcount and key hires/departures
- Technical performance metrics (uptime, latency, throughput)

### Section 3: Governance Summary

| Metric | Current Quarter | Prior Quarter | QoQ Change |
|--------|----------------|---------------|------------|
| **Proposals Submitted** | # | # | +/- |
| **Proposals Passed** | # | # | +/- |
| **Average Voter Participation** | % | % | +/- |
| **Unique Voters** | # | # | +/- |

**Additional governance disclosures:**
- Summary of material governance decisions and their rationale
- Delegation changes (new delegates, delegation shifts, concentration metrics)
- Governance process improvements implemented
- Upcoming proposals in discussion

### Section 4: Strategic Outlook

- **Market environment** -- macro trends, competitive landscape, sector dynamics
- **Competitive positioning** -- market share, differentiation, moat assessment
- **Strategic priorities** for the next quarter -- top 3-5 focus areas with measurable targets
- **Risk factors** -- key risks and mitigation strategies

{% hint style="success" %}
**Report Best Practices**

- **Standardize the format** -- use the same template every quarter for comparability
- **Include both absolute numbers and trends** -- stakeholders need both current state and trajectory
- **Be candid about setbacks** -- acknowledge what did not go well and what the DAO is doing about it
- **Link to on-chain data sources** -- provide Dune dashboard links, Etherscan addresses, and governance platform URLs for verification
- **Publish on a consistent date** -- e.g., the 15th of the month following quarter-end
{% endhint %}

## 16.3.3 Delegate Relations

In DAOs with significant delegation, a small number of delegates often control a disproportionate share of voting power. Effective delegate relations are essential for governance alignment, proposal passage, and institutional credibility.

### Delegate Mapping

Identify and categorize all delegates with meaningful governance influence.

| Category | Voting Power | Engagement Level | Priority |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Tier 1 (Key)** | >0.5% of total voting power | High -- regular 1:1 conversations | Highest |
| **Tier 2 (Important)** | 0.1-0.5% of total voting power | Moderate -- quarterly check-ins | High |
| **Tier 3 (Emerging)** | <0.1% but active and growing | Periodic -- monitor delegation trends | Medium |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Mapping Exercise**

For each Tier 1 delegate, document:
- **Delegate address** and governance platform profile
- **Current voting power** (owned + delegated) and trend
- **Voting history** on material proposals (supportive, opposing, abstaining)
- **Public positions** on key issues (e.g., treasury management, contributor compensation)
- **Communication preferences** (governance calls, 1:1s, forum engagement)
- **Delegators** -- who delegates to them and why

This mapping should be updated quarterly alongside the governance report.
{% endhint %}

### Delegate Engagement

Regular, proactive engagement with key delegates builds trust, surfaces concerns early, and improves proposal outcomes.

| Activity | Frequency | Format | Purpose |
|----------|-----------|--------|---------|
| **1:1 meetings** | Monthly (Tier 1) | Video call or in-person | Relationship building, feedback, alignment |
| **Delegate briefings** | Before major proposals | Written brief + call | Ensure delegates understand context before voting |
| **Governance calls** | Bi-weekly or monthly | Open community call | Broad delegate and community engagement |
| **Conference engagement** | Major events | In-person meetings | Deepen relationships, recruit new delegates |

### Delegate Incentivization

Governance participation requires meaningful time and expertise. Incentivizing delegates ensures sustained, high-quality engagement.

| Mechanism | Description | Typical Range |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Delegate stipends** | Fixed monthly compensation for active governance participation | $2,000-$10,000/month |
| **Performance grants** | Grants tied to participation metrics (proposals reviewed, votes cast) | $5,000-$25,000/quarter |
| **Retroactive rewards** | Recognition and compensation for exceptional governance contributions | Variable |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Incentive Design Pitfalls**

- **Avoid pure vote-count incentives** -- rewarding votes without quality assessment encourages rubber-stamping
- **Require written rationales** -- tie incentives to publishing voting rationales, not just casting votes
- **Monitor concentration** -- ensure incentive programs do not further concentrate governance power
- **Sunset provisions** -- include regular review periods to ensure the program remains effective
{% endhint %}

### Delegate Accountability

Tracking and publishing delegate voting records creates transparency and accountability.

**Key metrics to track:**

| Metric | Description | Benchmark |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| **Participation rate** | % of proposals voted on | >80% for stipend recipients |
| **Rationale publication** | % of votes with published rationale | >50% for Tier 1 delegates |
| **Delegation retention** | Change in delegated voting power over time | Stable or growing |
| **Forum engagement** | Posts, comments, and proposal discussions | Regular activity |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Tools for Delegate Tracking**

- **Tally** -- comprehensive governance analytics and delegate profiles
- **Boardroom** -- cross-protocol governance data and delegate rankings
- **Snapshot** -- off-chain voting with delegate tracking
- **Karma** -- delegate reputation and activity scoring
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- DAO IR serves a broader constituency than traditional IR -- token holders, delegates, institutional investors, partners, contributors, and community

- The five core IR activities are financial reporting, governance communication, strategic updates, investor engagement, and crisis communication

- The quarterly governance report is the flagship document -- covering financials, operations, governance, and strategic outlook in a standardized, comparable format

- Delegate relations require systematic mapping (prioritized by voting power), proactive engagement (regular 1:1s and briefings), appropriate incentivization (stipends tied to quality, not quantity), and accountability (published voting records and participation metrics)

- Crisis communication demands speed and transparency -- acknowledge immediately, update regularly, deliver root cause analysis, and follow up on remediation

- Consistency and candor are the foundations of credible IR -- a DAO that publishes reliable reports and acknowledges setbacks honestly builds more institutional trust than one that publishes only positive updates sporadically
