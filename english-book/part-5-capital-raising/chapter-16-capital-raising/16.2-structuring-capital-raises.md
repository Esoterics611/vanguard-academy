# 16.2 Structuring Capital Raises

Structuring a DAO capital raise requires translating traditional capital markets concepts -- pricing, lock-ups, covenants, conversion mechanics -- into the on-chain governance context. This section provides the detailed structuring frameworks for the three most important DAO capital raising instruments: OTC token sales, protocol bonds, and convertible notes.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Structure an OTC token sale with appropriate size, pricing, lock-up, and governance terms
- Apply discount benchmarks calibrated to lock-up periods and market conditions
- Design a protocol bond with coupon, maturity, collateral, and covenant provisions
- Analyze bond economics relative to equity cost of capital
- Structure a convertible note with conversion triggers, discounts, and valuation caps
- Calculate conversion economics under different FDV scenarios
{% endhint %}

## 16.2.1 OTC Token Sale Structuring

The OTC token sale is the most common DAO capital raising mechanism. The investment banker structures the sale across four key parameters: size, pricing, lock-up/vesting, and governance rights.

### Sale Parameters

#### Size

Limit any single OTC round to **1-3% of total token supply**. Larger rounds risk excessive dilution, community backlash, and concentrated governance power in a single investor.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Sizing Discipline**

Exceeding 3% of supply in a single round creates multiple risks:
- **Governance concentration** -- a single investor with 3%+ voting power can influence proposals unilaterally
- **Market overhang** -- large blocks coming out of lock-up depress prices
- **Community sentiment** -- perceived "sell-out" to institutional interests
- **Precedent** -- sets expectations for future rounds at unfavorable sizes

If the DAO needs more capital than 3% provides, structure as multiple sequential rounds with different investors.
{% endhint %}

#### Pricing

Price OTC sales at a **discount to trailing VWAP** (volume-weighted average price). The VWAP window is typically 7-day, 14-day, or 30-day, with longer windows providing more stable (and generally higher) reference prices.

```
OTC Sale Price = VWAP(window) x (1 - Discount%)

Example:
  30-day VWAP = $5.00
  Discount = 15%
  OTC Price = $5.00 x (1 - 0.15) = $4.25 per token
```

### Discount Benchmarks

Discounts compensate investors for illiquidity during the lock-up period. Longer lock-ups command larger discounts.

| Lock-up Period | Discount to VWAP | Rationale |
|---------------|------------------|-----------|
| **6 months** | 5-15% | Short lock; limited illiquidity risk |
| **12 months** | 10-20% | Standard institutional lock; moderate risk |
| **18 months** | 15-25% | Extended lock; significant illiquidity premium |
| **24 months** | 20-30% | Full cycle exposure; highest illiquidity premium |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Market Cycle Adjustment**

In **bull markets**, discounts compress toward the lower end of each range -- investors accept smaller discounts because they expect continued price appreciation. In **bear markets**, discounts widen toward the upper end -- investors demand larger discounts to compensate for downside risk and reduced liquidity.

The investment banker should benchmark against recent comparable OTC deals (check Nansen, Arkham, and governance forum disclosures) and adjust for current market conditions.
{% endhint %}

### Lock-up and Vesting

**Recommended structure:** 6-12 month hard lock-up, followed by 6-12 months of linear vesting. Total lock-up plus vesting period: 12-24 months.

| Phase | Duration | Mechanism | Purpose |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------|
| **Hard Lock-up** | 6-12 months | No transfers, no sales | Ensures long-term alignment |
| **Linear Vesting** | 6-12 months | Tokens unlock proportionally (e.g., monthly or daily) | Prevents sudden supply shock |
| **Full Unlock** | End of vesting | All tokens transferable | Investor realizes full liquidity |

```
Example: 12-month lock + 12-month linear vest
  Months 0-12:   0% unlocked (hard lock)
  Month 13:      ~8.3% unlocked (1/12 of total)
  Month 14:      ~16.7% unlocked (2/12 of total)
  ...
  Month 24:      100% unlocked
```

{% hint style="success" %}
**Smart Contract Enforcement**

Lock-up and vesting should be enforced on-chain through vesting contracts (Sablier, Superfluid, or custom implementations). On-chain enforcement is:
- **Trustless** -- neither party can violate the terms
- **Transparent** -- community can verify compliance
- **Automatic** -- no manual token releases required

Never rely on off-chain agreements alone for lock-up enforcement.
{% endhint %}

### Governance Rights During Lock-up

**Recommended:** Allow governance voting during lock-up to align incentives and encourage engagement.

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| **Full voting during lock-up** | Aligns incentives; encourages engagement; investor adds governance value | Governance influence before economic commitment proven |
| **No voting during lock-up** | Prevents governance capture by locked investors | Misaligned incentives; investor has no influence over capital deployment |
| **Delegated voting** | Compromise; investor delegates to approved delegate | Adds complexity; may reduce investor engagement |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Best Practice**

Most well-structured OTC deals grant full governance rights during lock-up. This encourages investors to participate constructively in governance rather than passively waiting for unlock. The investor has a strong economic interest in good governance outcomes -- denying voting rights wastes this alignment.
{% endhint %}

## 16.2.2 Protocol Bond Design

Protocol bonds are debt instruments backed by the protocol's future revenue. They provide **non-dilutive capital** -- the DAO raises funds without selling governance tokens. For established protocols with predictable revenue streams, bonds can be significantly cheaper than equity (token sales).

### Bond Structure Parameters

| Parameter | Description | Typical Range |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Principal** | Amount raised in stablecoins or ETH | $5M-$50M |
| **Coupon** | Annual interest rate | 8-15% (investment-grade); 15-25% (growth-stage) |
| **Maturity** | Bond term until principal repayment | 1-3 years |
| **Collateral** | Assets securing the bond | Treasury assets, revenue, native tokens |
| **Covenants** | Protective conditions | Min treasury balance, max debt, revenue coverage |
| **Implementation** | Execution mechanism | On-chain smart contract with automatic coupon payments |

### Coupon Rate Benchmarks

The coupon rate reflects the protocol's credit quality -- its ability to generate sufficient revenue to service the debt.

| Protocol Tier | Examples | Coupon Range | Revenue Coverage Required |
|--------------|----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Investment-grade** | Aave, Lido, MakerDAO-tier | 8-15% | 3x+ annual coupon from net income |
| **Growth-stage** | Newer protocols with revenue | 15-25% | 2x+ annual coupon from revenue |
| **Early-stage** | Pre-revenue or minimal revenue | Generally not suitable for bonds | N/A |

### Collateral and Security

| Security Level | Description | Coupon Impact |
|---------------|-------------|--------------|
| **Fully secured** | Specific treasury assets pledged (1.5x+ collateral ratio) | Lower coupon |
| **Partially secured** | Revenue pledge plus partial treasury backing | Moderate coupon |
| **Unsecured** | Backed only by protocol creditworthiness | Higher coupon |

### Covenant Design

Covenants protect bondholders by requiring the DAO to maintain minimum financial health standards.

| Covenant Type | Example | Trigger |
|--------------|---------|---------|
| **Minimum treasury balance** | Treasury must stay above $50M | Below threshold triggers accelerated repayment |
| **Maximum debt limit** | Total debt cannot exceed 30% of treasury | Prevents over-leveraging |
| **Revenue coverage ratio** | Revenue must cover coupon by 2x+ | Below ratio restricts new debt |
| **Governance approval** | Material changes require bondholder consent | Protects against adverse governance actions |

### Case Study: Hypothetical Bond Issuance for a Major Lending Protocol

{% hint style="info" %}
**Protocol Profile**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total Value Locked (TVL) | $500M |
| Annual Revenue | $60M |
| Net Income | $40M |
| Stablecoin Treasury | $100M |
| Credit Quality | Investment-grade |
{% endhint %}

**Proposed Bond Terms:**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Principal** | $20M USDC |
| **Coupon** | 10% annual (paid quarterly) |
| **Maturity** | 2 years |
| **Collateral** | $30M stablecoin collateral (1.5x coverage) |
| **Covenants** | Min $50M treasury; Min 2x revenue-to-coupon ratio |
| **Implementation** | On-chain with automatic quarterly coupon payments |

**Economic Analysis:**

```
Annual Coupon Payment:
  $20M x 10% = $2M per year
  Quarterly payment = $500K

Coupon as % of Revenue:
  $2M / $60M = 3.3% of annual revenue

Coupon as % of Net Income:
  $2M / $40M = 5.0% of net income

Revenue Coverage Ratio:
  $60M / $2M = 30x  (well above 2x covenant)

Net Income Coverage Ratio:
  $40M / $2M = 20x  (comfortably serviceable)
```

{% hint style="success" %}
**Debt vs. Equity Cost Comparison**

The 10% bond coupon is substantially cheaper than the implied cost of equity for most protocols:

| Capital Source | Cost | Dilution |
|---------------|------|----------|
| **Protocol bond (10% coupon)** | $2M/year for 2 years | None |
| **OTC token sale (20% discount)** | 20% of sold tokens' value | 1-3% of supply |
| **Implied cost of equity** | 25-40% for most protocols | Direct ownership transfer |

For established protocols with strong revenue, **debt is almost always cheaper than equity**. The investment banker should present this analysis to governance when recommending bond issuance.
{% endhint %}

## 16.2.3 Convertible Note Design

Convertible notes are hybrid instruments that combine debt characteristics (principal, interest) with equity optionality (conversion to governance tokens). They are particularly useful for growth-stage protocols that may not yet have sufficient revenue to support bonds, but where investors believe in future value appreciation.

### Conversion Triggers

Conversion from debt to tokens can be triggered by three mechanisms:

| Trigger Type | Description | When Used |
|-------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Time-based** | Converts at maturity date | Default mechanism; simple and predictable |
| **Price-based** | Converts when token price hits threshold | Aligns conversion with value creation milestones |
| **Event-based** | Converts upon specific events (new funding round, TGE, listing) | Common for pre-token protocols |

### Conversion Terms

Two key parameters protect both the investor and the DAO from adverse conversion outcomes:

**Conversion Discount:** The investor converts at a discount to market price at the time of conversion, compensating for early-stage risk.

```
Conversion Price = Market Price at Conversion x (1 - Discount%)

Typical range: 15-30% discount
```

**Valuation Cap:** A maximum valuation at which conversion occurs, protecting the investor if the protocol appreciates significantly before conversion.

```
Conversion at Cap = Investment Amount / Valuation Cap
```

The investor receives whichever conversion rate is more favorable (more tokens).

### Worked Example: Convertible Note with Valuation Cap

{% hint style="info" %}
**Note Terms**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Principal** | $5M |
| **Valuation Cap** | $100M FDV |
| **Conversion Discount** | 20% |
| **Maturity** | 2 years |
{% endhint %}

**Scenario: FDV at maturity = $500M**

```
Conversion at Cap:
  $5M / $100M = 5.0% of token supply

Conversion at Discount:
  Effective FDV = $500M x (1 - 0.20) = $400M
  $5M / $400M = 1.25% of token supply

Investor takes the better rate (cap): 5.0% of supply
```

**Scenario: FDV at maturity = $80M**

```
Conversion at Cap:
  $5M / $100M = 5.0% of token supply

Conversion at Discount:
  Effective FDV = $80M x (1 - 0.20) = $64M
  $5M / $64M = 7.81% of token supply

Investor takes the better rate (discount): 7.81% of supply
```

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Dilution Risk from Convertible Notes**

The scenarios above illustrate the dilution risk. If the token price declines significantly, the discount-based conversion can result in substantial dilution (7.81% in the bear case vs. 1.25% if the protocol appreciates). The investment banker must model multiple scenarios and ensure governance understands the worst-case dilution outcomes before approving convertible note terms.

**Mitigation:** Set a conversion floor (minimum FDV for conversion) to prevent extreme dilution in adverse scenarios. If the floor is breached, the note repays as straight debt.
{% endhint %}

### Convertible Note Summary

| Parameter | Typical Range | Negotiation Guidance |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Principal** | $1M-$20M | Size to bridge to next milestone |
| **Interest Rate** | 5-10% annually | Lower for stronger protocols |
| **Conversion Discount** | 15-30% | Benchmark against OTC discounts |
| **Valuation Cap** | 2-5x current FDV | Protect against excessive dilution |
| **Maturity** | 1-2 years | Align with milestone timeline |
| **Conversion Floor** | 0.5-0.75x current FDV | Prevent extreme bear case dilution |

---

## Key Takeaways

- OTC token sales should be limited to 1-3% of supply per round, priced at a discount to VWAP benchmarked against lock-up period (5-30% depending on duration)

- Lock-up and vesting should total 12-24 months, enforced on-chain through smart contracts -- never rely on off-chain agreements alone

- Protocol bonds provide non-dilutive capital at 8-15% coupon for investment-grade protocols -- substantially cheaper than the 25-40% implied cost of equity

- Bond covenants (minimum treasury, debt limits, revenue coverage) protect bondholders while maintaining DAO operational flexibility

- Convertible notes defer dilution but create uncertainty -- the investment banker must model best-case, base-case, and worst-case conversion scenarios

- A conversion floor protects the DAO from extreme dilution in bear market scenarios where discount-based conversion would be most punitive
