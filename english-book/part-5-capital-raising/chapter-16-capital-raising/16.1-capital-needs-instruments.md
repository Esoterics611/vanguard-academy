# 16.1 Capital Needs and Instruments

Unlike traditional companies that routinely access public capital markets through equity offerings and debt issuance, DAOs have historically relied on two primary capital sources: protocol revenue and native token sales. This limited capital structure toolkit constrains the DAO's ability to fund growth, execute acquisitions, invest in strategic initiatives, and maintain competitive positioning. The investment banker's role in DAO capital raising is to expand this toolkit -- providing the DAO with access to a broader range of capital sources and structuring capital raises that minimize dilution while meeting the DAO's funding needs.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Conduct a capital needs assessment across four capital categories
- Identify when and why a DAO should raise external capital
- Evaluate the full menu of capital raising instruments available to DAOs
- Compare instruments by dilution impact, cost, risk profile, and best use case
{% endhint %}

## 16.1.1 Capital Needs Assessment

The capital needs assessment answers three fundamental questions:

1. **How much capital does the DAO need?**
2. **For what purpose?**
3. **Over what timeframe?**

These questions map to four distinct capital categories, each with different urgency, size, and structuring implications.

### Growth Capital

Funding for scaling the protocol -- hiring additional contributors, expanding to new chains, launching new products, and increasing marketing and business development efforts.

Growth capital needs are typically:
- **Predictable** -- tied to a roadmap or strategic plan
- **Flexible in timing** -- can be staged over multiple quarters
- **Moderate in size** -- 5-15% of treasury value per year

{% hint style="info" %}
**Growth Capital Examples**

- Hiring 20 additional protocol engineers ($3-5M annually)
- Multi-chain deployment across 4 new L2s ($2-4M for audits, deployment, liquidity)
- Marketing and BD expansion into institutional markets ($1-2M)
- New product development (lending module, derivatives, RWA integration)
{% endhint %}

### Strategic Capital

Funding for specific strategic initiatives -- acquisitions, large partnership commitments, protocol-owned liquidity deployment, or ecosystem incentive programs. These needs are event-driven and often time-sensitive.

Strategic capital needs are typically:
- **Lumpy** -- large, one-time commitments
- **Time-sensitive** -- competitive dynamics require speed
- **Significant in size** -- often 10-30% of treasury value

### Defensive Capital

Building the treasury war chest to survive adverse market conditions -- bear markets, competitive attacks, or regulatory challenges.

Defensive capital needs are typically:
- **Proactive** -- best raised before the crisis arrives
- **Counter-cyclical** -- hardest to raise when most needed
- **Sized to runway** -- targeting 18-24+ months of operating expenses in stablecoins

{% hint style="warning" %}
**The Timing Paradox**

Defensive capital is most valuable when raised proactively during bull markets. DAOs that wait until bear markets face wider discounts, reduced investor appetite, and depressed token prices -- exactly when they need capital most. The investment banker should advocate for building defensive reserves during periods of strength.
{% endhint %}

### Working Capital

Ensuring the DAO has sufficient liquid assets to meet ongoing operational obligations. If the operating reserve falls below the minimum threshold (12 months of expenses), replenishment is a priority.

Working capital needs are typically:
- **Continuous** -- monitored monthly against threshold targets
- **Non-negotiable** -- falling below minimum threatens operations
- **Moderate in size** -- calibrated to monthly burn rate

### Capital Needs Summary

| Category | Timing | Typical Size | Urgency | Best Instruments |
|----------|--------|-------------|---------|------------------|
| **Growth** | Planned, staged | 5-15% of treasury/year | Medium | OTC sales, grants |
| **Strategic** | Event-driven | 10-30% of treasury | High | OTC sales, bonds, token swaps |
| **Defensive** | Proactive | 18-24 months runway | Low (if proactive) | OTC sales, bonds |
| **Working** | Continuous | Monthly burn x 12 | Critical if below threshold | Revenue, small OTC sales |

## 16.1.2 Capital Raising Options for DAOs

The DAO investment banker has seven primary instruments available, each with distinct trade-offs across dilution, cost, complexity, and suitability.

### Treasury Token Sales (OTC)

Selling native tokens from the treasury to institutional investors through negotiated OTC (over-the-counter) transactions. This is the **most common** capital raising mechanism for DAOs.

**How it works:** The DAO governance approves the sale of a defined number of treasury tokens to one or more institutional buyers at a negotiated discount to market price, subject to lock-up and vesting restrictions.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | Direct -- typically 1-3% of total supply per round |
| **Cost** | 10-25% discount to VWAP (varies by lock-up) |
| **Complexity** | Moderate -- requires governance approval, legal structuring |
| **Best for** | Most capital needs; building institutional relationships |
| **Risk to DAO** | Selling pressure post-unlock; community sentiment backlash |

### Revenue Bonds / Protocol Bonds

Issuing debt instruments backed by the protocol's future revenue. The DAO receives capital upfront and commits to repaying the principal plus interest from future protocol revenue.

**How it works:** The DAO issues on-chain bonds with defined principal, coupon rate, maturity, and collateral. Smart contracts automate coupon payments from protocol revenue.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | None -- pure debt instrument |
| **Cost** | 8-15% coupon (investment-grade); 15-25% (growth-stage) |
| **Complexity** | High -- requires revenue history, smart contract implementation |
| **Best for** | Established protocols with predictable revenue streams |
| **Risk to DAO** | Fixed obligation regardless of revenue fluctuations |

### Convertible Notes

Hybrid instruments combining debt and equity characteristics. The investor provides capital for a note that accrues interest and can be converted to governance tokens at a predetermined rate or discount.

**How it works:** The investor lends capital at a stated interest rate. At maturity or upon trigger events, the note converts to governance tokens at a discount to market price, subject to a valuation cap.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | Deferred -- occurs at conversion, not issuance |
| **Cost** | 15-30% conversion discount plus interest accrual |
| **Complexity** | High -- conversion mechanics, cap calculations |
| **Best for** | Pre-revenue or growth-stage protocols; bridge financing |
| **Risk to DAO** | Dilution at potentially unfavorable terms if token price appreciates significantly |

### Revenue Sharing Agreements

The DAO sells a percentage of future revenue for upfront capital. Similar to revenue-based financing in traditional markets. The investor receives a share of protocol revenue until achieving a 2-5x return on their investment.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | None -- no token issuance |
| **Cost** | 2-5x return multiple on invested capital |
| **Complexity** | Moderate -- revenue oracle integration, payment automation |
| **Best for** | Protocols with predictable, growing revenue |
| **Risk to DAO** | Long-term revenue commitment; reduces treasury inflows |

### Strategic Token Swaps

Exchanging native tokens with another DAO to create mutual economic alignment. Both DAOs gain governance participation and economic exposure to the partner protocol.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | Mutual -- both parties exchange tokens |
| **Cost** | Opportunity cost of tokens exchanged |
| **Complexity** | Moderate -- bilateral negotiation, governance approval on both sides |
| **Best for** | Building strategic partnerships and ecosystem alignment |
| **Risk to DAO** | Partner protocol performance; governance participation complexity |

### Grant Funding

Applying for grants from larger ecosystem funds (Ethereum Foundation, Optimism RetroPGF, Arbitrum ecosystem grants). Completely non-dilutive.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | None |
| **Cost** | Application effort; milestone compliance |
| **Complexity** | Low-moderate -- application and reporting requirements |
| **Best for** | Public goods, ecosystem tooling, early-stage protocols |
| **Risk to DAO** | Uncertain timing; may come with usage restrictions |

### Protocol-Owned Liquidity Revenue

Deploying POL positions that generate trading fee revenue. While not a traditional capital raise, POL can generate significant ongoing revenue that reduces the need for external capital.

| Attribute | Detail |
|-----------|--------|
| **Dilution** | None -- uses existing treasury assets |
| **Cost** | Impermanent loss risk; opportunity cost of deployed capital |
| **Complexity** | Moderate -- requires active position management |
| **Best for** | Supplementing treasury revenue; reducing reliance on external capital |
| **Risk to DAO** | IL in volatile markets; smart contract risk of LP venues |

## Capital Raising Instruments Comparison

{% hint style="success" %}
**Instrument Selection Framework**

When advising a DAO on capital raising, the investment banker should evaluate instruments across five dimensions:

1. **Dilution impact** -- How much ownership do existing holders give up?
2. **Cost of capital** -- What is the all-in cost (discount, coupon, multiple)?
3. **Execution complexity** -- How difficult is governance approval, legal structuring, and implementation?
4. **Strategic value** -- Does the instrument bring non-capital benefits (partnerships, expertise, governance engagement)?
5. **Market conditions** -- Which instruments are most favorable given current sentiment and liquidity?
{% endhint %}

| Instrument | Dilution | Cost of Capital | Complexity | Non-Capital Benefits | Market Sensitivity |
|-----------|----------|----------------|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| **OTC Token Sale** | High (direct) | Moderate (10-25% discount) | Moderate | Institutional relationships | High |
| **Protocol Bond** | None | Moderate (8-25% coupon) | High | Credibility signal | Moderate |
| **Convertible Note** | Deferred | High (discount + interest) | High | Flexible terms | Moderate |
| **Revenue Sharing** | None | High (2-5x multiple) | Moderate | Aligned incentives | Low |
| **Token Swap** | Mutual | Low (opportunity cost) | Moderate | Strategic partnership | Moderate |
| **Grant Funding** | None | Very Low | Low-Moderate | Ecosystem support | Low |
| **POL Revenue** | None | Variable (IL risk) | Moderate | Permanent liquidity | High |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Practical Guidance**

Most DAO capital raises use a combination of instruments. A typical approach might combine an OTC token sale (for immediate capital) with a protocol bond (for non-dilutive financing) and a strategic token swap (for partnership alignment). The investment banker's value lies in designing the optimal mix for the DAO's specific needs and market conditions.
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- The capital needs assessment categorizes funding requirements into four types: growth, strategic, defensive, and working capital -- each with distinct urgency, sizing, and instrument implications

- DAOs have seven primary capital raising instruments, ranging from dilutive (OTC token sales) to non-dilutive (bonds, grants, POL revenue)

- The most common mechanism is the OTC token sale, but sophisticated DAOs increasingly use protocol bonds and convertible notes to reduce dilution costs

- Defensive capital is best raised proactively during bull markets -- waiting until bear markets produces worse terms and may not be feasible at all

- The investment banker's role is to design the optimal instrument mix based on the DAO's capital needs, cost sensitivity, dilution tolerance, and market conditions
