# Chapter 16: Capital Raising Strategies for DAOs

Unlike traditional companies that routinely access public capital markets through equity offerings and debt issuance, DAOs have historically relied on two primary capital sources: protocol revenue and native token sales. This limited toolkit constrains the DAO's ability to fund growth, execute acquisitions, invest in strategic initiatives, and maintain competitive positioning. The investment banker's role is to expand this toolkit -- providing DAOs with access to a broader range of capital sources and structuring raises that minimize dilution while meeting funding needs.

## Chapter Overview

This chapter covers the full capital raising lifecycle for DAO investment bankers:

- **Capital needs assessment** -- identifying and categorizing a DAO's funding requirements across growth, strategic, defensive, and working capital
- **Capital raising instruments** -- OTC token sales, protocol bonds, convertible notes, revenue sharing agreements, strategic token swaps, and grant funding
- **OTC sale structuring** -- pricing against VWAP, discount benchmarks by lock-up period, vesting schedules, and governance rights
- **Protocol bond design** -- coupon rates, maturity, collateral, covenants, and smart contract implementation with a full case study
- **Convertible note design** -- conversion triggers, valuation caps, discount mechanics, and dilution analysis
- **Investor relations** -- the quarterly governance report, delegate mapping and engagement, crisis communication
- **Cap table design** -- ownership tracking across stakeholder classes, dilution analysis formulas, and dynamic modeling over 3-5 year horizons

## Key Concepts

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Capital Structure Constraints in DAOs**

DAOs cannot simply issue new equity classes or access traditional debt markets. Every capital raise involves trade-offs between dilution, cost of capital, governance implications, and community sentiment. The investment banker must navigate these constraints while structuring instruments that institutional investors recognize and trust -- bridging decentralized governance with institutional capital markets.
{% endhint %}
