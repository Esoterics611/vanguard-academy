# Chapter 17: Fundraising Execution and Process

The capital raising process for a DAO follows a structured sequence that adapts the traditional fundraising roadshow to the decentralized context. Where a conventional IPO roadshow involves management teams presenting to institutional investors behind closed doors, a DAO capital raise must navigate governance pre-approval, on-chain transparency, and pseudonymous core teams -- all while maintaining the professional rigor that institutional investors expect.

## Chapter Overview

This chapter provides the complete fundraising execution framework for DAO investment bankers:

- **The four-phase capital raising process** -- a structured 12-18 week timeline from strategic preparation through governance approval and transaction execution
- **Strategic preparation** -- capital needs assessment, instrument selection, term sheet drafting, and governance pre-approval
- **Investor engagement** -- target list construction, investment memoranda, roadshow management, and DAO-specific differences from traditional processes
- **Negotiation and structuring** -- lead investor negotiations, allocation management, final documentation
- **Governance approval and execution** -- final proposals, community voting, smart contract execution, and post-close communication
- **Ongoing capital markets advisory** -- market intelligence, investor feedback synthesis, capital structure optimization, and strategic financing advisory
- **Benchmarking and competitive intelligence** -- maintaining transaction databases and benchmarking across key dimensions

## Key Concepts

{% hint style="info" %}
**The DAO Capital Raising Timeline**

Unlike traditional fundraising, a DAO capital raise includes overlapping phases that account for governance process requirements. The typical timeline runs 12-18 weeks, with phases running in parallel during transition periods:

```
Phase 1 (Weeks 1-4):   Strategic Preparation
Phase 2 (Weeks 4-10):  Investor Engagement
Phase 3 (Weeks 8-14):  Negotiation & Structuring
Phase 4 (Weeks 12-18): Governance Approval & Execution
```
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Governance Pre-Approval Is Non-Negotiable**

Securing governance pre-approval before approaching investors is critical. It prevents the embarrassment and legal risk of negotiating a deal that governance subsequently rejects. It also demonstrates to investors that the DAO can execute decisions, reducing governance risk perception.
{% endhint %}
