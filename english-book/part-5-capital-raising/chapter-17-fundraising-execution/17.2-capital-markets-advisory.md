# 17.2 Capital Markets Advisory

Beyond discrete capital raising transactions, the investment banker provides ongoing capital markets advisory that helps the DAO maintain optimal capital structure, stay informed on market conditions, and prepare for future financing needs. The best investment bankers function as a continuous strategic resource -- anticipating needs before they become urgent rather than reacting to crises.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Deliver ongoing market intelligence on investor appetite, prevailing multiples, and regulatory developments
- Synthesize investor feedback to help DAOs understand external perception
- Conduct periodic capital structure optimization reviews
- Advise on timing and structure of future capital raises based on market conditions
- Build and maintain a benchmarking database of DAO capital raising transactions
- Apply competitive intelligence across six key benchmarking dimensions
{% endhint %}

---

## 17.2.1 Ongoing Capital Markets Advisory

Ongoing advisory transforms the investment banker from a transactional service provider into a strategic partner. Four core advisory functions provide continuous value between capital raising events.

### Market Intelligence

The investment banker provides regular updates on conditions that affect the DAO's capital markets positioning:

| Intelligence Category | Content | Frequency |
|----------------------|---------|-----------|
| **Institutional Investor Appetite** | Which funds are actively deploying, sector preferences, check sizes, risk appetite shifts | Monthly |
| **Prevailing Multiples** | Current valuation multiples across protocol categories (DEX, lending, L2, infrastructure) | Monthly |
| **Comparable Transaction Terms** | Recent OTC sales, bond issuances, and other capital raises with term details | As they occur |
| **Regulatory Developments** | New guidance, enforcement actions, or legislative proposals affecting token capital raising | As they occur |

{% hint style="success" %}
**Best Practice: Structured Market Updates**

Deliver market intelligence through a standardized monthly report with a consistent format. This allows governance participants and treasury committees to track trends over time rather than processing ad hoc updates. Include a "market conditions summary" with a clear recommendation on whether current conditions favor or disfavor capital raising activity.
{% endhint %}

### Investor Feedback

The investment banker serves as a critical conduit between the institutional investor community and the DAO, synthesizing feedback across three dimensions:

| Feedback Dimension | What It Reveals | How the DAO Can Respond |
|-------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| **Protocol Performance** | How investors evaluate on-chain metrics, revenue growth, and competitive positioning | Prioritize metrics that investors track; address underperformance narratives |
| **Governance Health** | Investor perception of governance participation, decision quality, and execution speed | Improve governance reporting, increase transparency, streamline processes |
| **Market Positioning** | How the protocol is perceived relative to competitors in investor portfolios | Sharpen competitive narrative, highlight differentiation, address perception gaps |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Investor Feedback Is a Mirror, Not a Mandate**

Investor feedback helps the DAO understand how it is perceived externally, but it should inform strategy rather than dictate it. DAOs that chase investor sentiment at the expense of community values risk alienating their core contributors and users. The investment banker should present feedback with context, distinguishing between actionable insights and noise.
{% endhint %}

### Capital Structure Optimization

Periodic review of the DAO's capital structure ensures it remains appropriate as the protocol evolves and market conditions change:

**Capital Structure Review Framework:**

| Review Area | Key Questions | Optimization Actions |
|-------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Token Supply Dynamics** | How are unlock schedules affecting circulating supply? Is inflation diluting value? | Adjust emission schedules, implement buyback programs, modify staking incentives |
| **Treasury Composition** | Is the treasury appropriately diversified? Is the operating reserve adequate? | Rebalance across the Three-Bucket Model, adjust stablecoin targets |
| **Debt/Equity Mix** | Has the protocol's credit quality improved enough to support debt instruments? | Evaluate bond issuance capacity, consider refinancing existing obligations |
| **Liquidity Position** | Is protocol-owned liquidity sufficient for token stability? | Deploy additional POL, adjust DEX liquidity incentives |

```
Capital Structure Review Cadence:
  Quarterly:  Treasury composition and runway assessment
  Semi-Annual: Full capital structure review with optimization recommendations
  Annual:     Comprehensive strategic financing plan for upcoming 12-month period
```

### Strategic Financing Advisory

Advising on the timing and structure of future capital raises requires anticipating needs before they become urgent:

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Critical: Proactive vs. Reactive Capital Raising**

DAOs that raise capital proactively -- during favorable market conditions with ample runway remaining -- preserve significantly more value than those forced to raise reactively during bear markets or liquidity crises. Reactive capital raising typically results in:
- **Wider discounts** (25-40% vs. 10-20% in favorable conditions)
- **Shorter lock-ups** demanded by investors (who have more leverage)
- **Weaker governance terms** (investors extract more concessions)
- **Rushed governance process** (increasing community backlash risk)

The best investment bankers anticipate capital needs 6-12 months in advance and recommend action during windows of strength.
{% endhint %}

**Strategic Financing Advisory Deliverables:**

| Deliverable | Content | When |
|-------------|---------|------|
| **Market Window Assessment** | Analysis of whether current conditions favor capital raising, with specific timing recommendations | Quarterly or when conditions shift materially |
| **Instrument Recommendation** | Updated analysis of optimal instrument type given current market conditions and protocol maturity | When conditions change or new instruments emerge |
| **Size and Pricing Guidance** | Recommended raise size and expected pricing based on current comparable transactions | Before any capital raise planning begins |
| **Investor Landscape Update** | Which investors are currently active, deploying capital, and interested in the DAO's sector | Quarterly |

## 17.2.2 Benchmarking and Competitive Intelligence

A robust benchmarking practice is the foundation of informed capital markets advisory. The investment banker should maintain a comprehensive database of DAO capital raising transactions -- tracking terms, pricing, investor participation, and outcomes.

### Transaction Database

The benchmarking database should capture every material DAO capital raising transaction with sufficient detail to enable meaningful comparison:

{% hint style="info" %}
**Transaction Database Fields**

For each recorded transaction, capture:
- **Protocol name and category** (DEX, lending, L2, infrastructure, etc.)
- **Transaction type** (OTC sale, bond, convertible note, revenue share)
- **Size** (USD value and token amount)
- **Pricing** (discount to VWAP, coupon rate, conversion terms)
- **Lock-up/vesting terms** (duration, cliff, schedule)
- **Governance rights granted** (if any)
- **Lead investor(s)** and other participants
- **Market conditions at time of close** (bull/bear, sector sentiment)
- **Post-transaction outcomes** (token price performance, governance participation)
{% endhint %}

### Benchmarking Dimensions

The database enables benchmarking across six key dimensions that inform pricing, structuring, and investor engagement:

#### 1. OTC Discount Levels

| Protocol Quality Tier | 6-Month Lock-up | 12-Month Lock-up | 18-Month Lock-up | 24-Month Lock-up |
|----------------------|----------------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Blue-chip** (top protocols by TVL/revenue) | 5-10% | 8-15% | 12-20% | 15-25% |
| **Growth-stage** (established but scaling) | 10-18% | 15-22% | 18-28% | 22-32% |
| **Early-stage** (pre-product-market fit) | 15-25% | 20-30% | 25-35% | 30-40% |

#### 2. Bond Coupon Rates

| Protocol Credit Quality | Coupon Range | Key Determinants |
|------------------------|-------------|------------------|
| **Investment-grade** (Aave, Lido, MakerDAO-tier) | 8-15% | Strong revenue, diversified treasury, governance maturity |
| **Growth-stage** | 15-25% | Growing but variable revenue, moderate treasury |
| **Speculative** | 25%+ or not viable | Insufficient revenue for debt service |

#### 3. Convertible Note Terms

| Parameter | Typical Range | Notes |
|-----------|--------------|-------|
| **Valuation caps** | 20-40% discount to current FDV | Protects investor from overpaying at conversion |
| **Discount rates** | 15-30% | Applied at conversion event |
| **Maturity** | 12-36 months | Longer maturities for earlier-stage protocols |
| **Conversion triggers** | Governance vote, TGE, time-based | Should be clearly defined and measurable |

#### 4. Revenue Sharing Multiples

| Protocol Type | Return Multiple | Typical Duration |
|--------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **High-revenue DeFi** (DEX, lending) | 2-3x | 2-4 years |
| **Moderate-revenue** (infrastructure, middleware) | 3-4x | 3-5 years |
| **Early-revenue** (emerging protocols) | 4-5x | 4-6 years |

#### 5. Round Sizes as Percentage of FDV

```
Typical Round Size Benchmarks:
  Blue-chip protocols:   0.5-2% of FDV
  Growth-stage:          2-5% of FDV
  Early-stage:           5-15% of FDV

Exceeding these ranges signals potential over-dilution and may
face governance resistance or investor concerns about cap table quality.
```

#### 6. Investor Participation Patterns

| Pattern | Intelligence Value |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Most active investors** | Which funds are deploying most frequently in the sector |
| **Typical check sizes** | Expected commitment ranges by fund |
| **Preferred deal terms** | Lock-up preferences, governance participation requirements |
| **Sector preferences** | Which protocol categories each fund favors |
| **Co-investment patterns** | Which funds frequently invest together (syndicate networks) |

{% hint style="success" %}
**Best Practice: Update the Database Continuously**

The benchmarking database is only valuable if it reflects current market conditions. Update it with every new transaction that becomes public (many DAO capital raises are disclosed through governance proposals). Cross-reference on-chain data with governance forum disclosures to capture the most complete picture of each transaction.
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

&#x2705; Ongoing capital markets advisory transforms the investment banker from a transactional service provider into a strategic partner, providing continuous value between capital raising events

&#x2705; Four core advisory functions drive ongoing value: market intelligence, investor feedback synthesis, capital structure optimization, and strategic financing advisory

&#x2705; Market intelligence should be delivered through standardized monthly reports covering investor appetite, prevailing multiples, comparable transactions, and regulatory developments

&#x2705; Investor feedback helps the DAO understand external perception but should inform strategy rather than dictate it

&#x2705; Capital structure reviews should occur quarterly (treasury composition), semi-annually (full review), and annually (strategic financing plan)

&#x2705; Proactive capital raising during favorable conditions preserves significantly more value than reactive raises during crises

&#x2705; The benchmarking database should capture every material DAO capital raising transaction across six dimensions: OTC discounts, bond coupons, convertible note terms, revenue sharing multiples, round sizes as percentage of FDV, and investor participation patterns

&#x2705; Continuous database maintenance ensures advisory recommendations reflect current market conditions rather than stale data
