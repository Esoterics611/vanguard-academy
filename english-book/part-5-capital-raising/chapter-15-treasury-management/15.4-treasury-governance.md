# 15.4 Treasury Governance and Oversight

Even the most sophisticated asset allocation and risk management frameworks are ineffective without proper governance structures to oversee their implementation. Treasury governance defines who makes treasury decisions, what authority they have, how they report to the broader governance community, and what accountability mechanisms ensure responsible stewardship.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Design a Treasury Management Committee with appropriate membership and authority structure
- Distinguish between delegated authority (day-to-day) and restricted authority (governance-required)
- Implement treasury reporting standards across four cadences (monthly, quarterly, annual, ad hoc)
- Analyze MakerDAO's four-phase treasury evolution and extract key lessons for treasury governance design
{% endhint %}

---

## 15.4.1 The Treasury Management Committee

Effective DAO treasury management requires a dedicated committee with clearly defined authority. The committee operates as a delegate of the broader governance community -- executing treasury strategy within approved parameters while escalating decisions that exceed its mandate.

### Committee Structure

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Size** | 5-7 members |
| **Term** | 6-12 months, with staggered terms to ensure continuity |
| **Composition** | Mix of internal (core contributors, delegates) and external (independent advisors, institutional investors) |
| **Quorum** | Majority (e.g., 4 of 7) for routine decisions |
| **Meeting Cadence** | Weekly operational meetings; monthly strategic reviews |
| **Compensation** | Commensurate with responsibility and market rates for treasury/portfolio management |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Composition Matters**

The committee should include members with diverse expertise:
- **DeFi-native investment experience** -- understanding of on-chain yield strategies, smart contract risk, and market dynamics
- **Traditional finance background** -- institutional asset management discipline, risk frameworks, and reporting standards
- **Governance expertise** -- navigating DAO governance processes, building community consensus, and managing stakeholder communication
- **Technical competence** -- multi-sig operations, smart contract interaction, and operational security

Avoid committees composed entirely of insiders or entirely of external advisors. The best committees combine internal protocol knowledge with external professional discipline.
{% endhint %}

### Delegated vs. Restricted Authority

The committee's authority should be clearly divided into two categories:

#### Delegated Authority (Day-to-Day Operations)

The committee can execute these actions without a governance vote, provided they fall within approved parameters:

| Action | Parameters |
|--------|------------|
| **Rebalancing between buckets** | Within approved allocation ranges (e.g., +/- 5% of target) |
| **Deploying to approved protocols** | Pre-approved protocol whitelist; position size limits |
| **Executing approved selling programs** | Within governance-approved rate and schedule |
| **Routine OTC negotiations** | Within pre-approved discount ranges and counterparty lists |
| **Risk limit adjustments** | Minor adjustments within approved bands |
| **Emergency withdrawals** | From compromised protocols (immediate safety action) |

#### Restricted Authority (Governance Vote Required)

These actions exceed the committee's delegated authority and require a full governance vote:

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **New asset class introduction** | Adding RWAs, new chain deployments, or novel instruments requires community approval |
| **Exceeding concentration limits** | Temporary or permanent limit changes affect overall risk profile |
| **Large OTC transactions** | Sales exceeding a threshold (e.g., >$10M or >2% of native supply) require governance visibility |
| **Changes to Operating Reserve minimum** | Drawing down the survival guarantee requires explicit acknowledgment |
| **Committee membership changes** | Additions, removals, or authority changes |
| **Strategy changes** | Fundamental shifts in allocation targets, risk tolerance, or yield strategy |
| **New counterparty relationships** | Engaging with new OTC desks, RWA issuers, or custodians above a materiality threshold |

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Authority Creep**

One of the most common governance failures is gradual expansion of delegated authority without explicit governance approval. The committee should strictly limit itself to its approved mandate. If a situation requires action outside approved parameters, the committee should seek a governance vote -- even if this introduces delay. Unauthorized authority expansion erodes community trust and can result in committee removal.
{% endhint %}

## 15.4.2 Treasury Reporting Standards

Transparent, comprehensive reporting is the foundation of treasury governance accountability. The reporting framework should operate at four cadences:

### Monthly Report (Due Within 5 Business Days of Month-End)

| Component | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Treasury Summary** | Total value, change from prior month, composition breakdown |
| **Bucket Status** | Each bucket's value, allocation vs. target, and any limit breaches |
| **Transaction Log** | All treasury transactions executed during the month |
| **Performance** | Return by bucket and strategy vs. benchmark |
| **Risk Metrics** | VaR, concentration analysis, liquidity coverage |
| **Upcoming Actions** | Planned transactions, rebalancing, or governance proposals for the coming month |

### Quarterly Strategic Review

| Component | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Strategic Assessment** | Market conditions, macro outlook, and implications for treasury strategy |
| **Performance Attribution** | Detailed return analysis by asset class, strategy, and position |
| **Stress Test Results** | Updated quarterly stress testing across four standard scenarios |
| **Allocation Review** | Recommended changes to target allocations or risk parameters |
| **Committee Self-Assessment** | Governance process effectiveness, lessons learned, improvement areas |
| **Community Call** | Live presentation to governance community with Q&A |

{% hint style="success" %}
**Community Engagement**

The quarterly strategic review should include a live community call where the treasury committee presents results, answers questions, and solicits feedback. This is not optional -- it is the primary mechanism through which the governance community exercises oversight. Committees that avoid community engagement or provide opaque reporting will lose their mandate.
{% endhint %}

### Annual Audit

| Component | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Independent Review** | Third-party audit of treasury holdings, transactions, and compliance with approved parameters |
| **Reconciliation** | On-chain verification of all reported holdings |
| **Process Review** | Assessment of governance processes, risk management effectiveness, and operational security |
| **Recommendations** | Auditor's recommendations for improvements |

### Ad Hoc Reporting (Material Events)

Ad hoc reports are triggered by material events that require immediate governance awareness:

- Smart contract exploit affecting a treasury position
- Stablecoin depeg event
- Counterparty failure or solvency concern
- Regulatory action affecting treasury assets
- Concentration limit breach
- Operating reserve falling below minimum threshold

{% hint style="danger" %}
**No Surprises**

The cardinal rule of treasury reporting is: **no surprises**. The governance community should never learn about a material treasury event from external sources before hearing from the treasury committee. Ad hoc reporting should be immediate (within hours of the event), factual, and include a preliminary assessment of impact and response plan.
{% endhint %}

## 15.4.3 Case Study: MakerDAO's Treasury Management Evolution

MakerDAO's treasury management journey from 2018 to 2024 illustrates the evolution from minimal treasury oversight to institutional-grade management. It serves as both an instructive example and a cautionary tale for other DAOs.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Protocol Snapshot**

| Attribute | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Protocol** | MakerDAO (rebranded as Sky in 2024) |
| **Treasury Scale** | $1B+ in non-native treasury assets at peak |
| **Key Innovation** | First major DAO to allocate treasury to real-world assets (U.S. Treasury bills) |
| **Revenue Impact** | RWA allocations generated $50-100M+ in annual yield |
| **Governance Model** | MKR token governance with delegated committees |
{% endhint %}

### Phase 1: Minimal Treasury Management (2018-2020)

**Context:** MakerDAO's early treasury management was ad hoc. The protocol's reserves consisted primarily of accumulated stability fees held in DAI, with no formal allocation strategy, no treasury committee, and no systematic risk management.

**Characteristics:**
- Treasury held almost entirely in DAI and MKR
- No formal asset allocation framework
- Decisions made through general governance proposals on an as-needed basis
- Limited financial reporting

**Lesson:** In the protocol's early stage, the simplicity was appropriate -- the treasury was small and the focus was on protocol development. But this approach does not scale.

### Phase 2: Governance-Driven Diversification (2020-2021)

**Context:** As MakerDAO's revenue grew and the treasury accumulated significant DAI reserves, the community recognized the need for more sophisticated management. The bull market provided an opportune environment for diversification.

**Characteristics:**
- Introduction of the Strategic Finance Core Unit
- Beginning of formal treasury reporting
- Initial discussions of non-crypto asset allocation
- Community debates about risk tolerance and return objectives

**Lesson:** Governance-driven treasury discussions are slow but essential. Building community consensus on treasury strategy before deploying capital prevents contentious retroactive debates.

### Phase 3: RWA Integration and Institutional Scale (2022-2023)

**Context:** MakerDAO became the first major DAO to allocate treasury reserves at scale to real-world assets, primarily U.S. Treasury bills through institutional counterparties like BlockTower and Monetalis.

**Characteristics:**
- $1B+ allocated to U.S. Treasury bills and institutional credit
- Generated approximately 4-5% annualized yield on previously idle reserves
- Established institutional counterparty relationships
- Created sophisticated risk management and reporting frameworks
- Engaged professional treasury management advisors

**Lesson:** RWA integration at institutional scale requires extensive due diligence, legal structuring, and governance infrastructure. MakerDAO's success demonstrated that DAOs can access institutional-quality investment opportunities, but the operational overhead is significant.

{% hint style="success" %}
**Scale Advantage**

MakerDAO's $1B+ RWA allocation generated tens of millions of dollars in annual yield -- revenue that funded protocol development, contributor compensation, and further treasury growth. This demonstrated that professional treasury management is not just a cost center but a significant revenue source for well-capitalized DAOs.
{% endhint %}

### Phase 4: Endgame and SubDAO-Level Management (2023-2024)

**Context:** The "Endgame" restructuring plan proposed by founder Rune Christensen introduced SubDAOs, each with their own treasury and management authority. Treasury management devolved from a single centralized function to a multi-entity structure.

**Characteristics:**
- SubDAO-level treasury management with separate mandates
- Rebranding (SKY replacing MKR, USDS replacing DAI)
- Increased complexity of treasury oversight across multiple entities
- Continued RWA strategy but with decentralized execution

**Lesson:** Decentralizing treasury management across SubDAOs introduces coordination challenges and potential for inconsistent risk management. The benefits of specialization must be weighed against the costs of fragmentation.

### Key Lessons from MakerDAO's Evolution

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Four Lessons for DAO Treasury Governance**

1. **Start simple, scale with complexity** -- Early-stage DAOs should not over-engineer treasury governance. Start with basic allocation and reporting, then add sophistication as the treasury grows.

2. **Build governance consensus before deploying capital** -- Community debates about treasury strategy are time-consuming but essential. Deploying capital without governance consensus creates political risk that can unwind the strategy.

3. **Professional management pays for itself** -- The yield generated by professional treasury management ($50-100M+ annually for MakerDAO) far exceeds the cost of the treasury team and infrastructure. Under-investing in treasury management is a false economy.

4. **Diversify before you need to** -- MakerDAO's early RWA allocations proved prescient during the 2022 bear market, providing stable yield when crypto-native revenue sources declined. DAOs that wait until bear markets to diversify face worse terms and limited options.
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

✅ The Treasury Management Committee should have 5-7 members with staggered 6-12 month terms, combining DeFi-native, traditional finance, governance, and technical expertise

✅ Authority must be clearly divided: delegated authority for day-to-day operations within approved parameters, restricted authority requiring governance votes for strategic changes

✅ Treasury reporting operates at four cadences: monthly operational reports (within 5 days), quarterly strategic reviews (with community calls), annual independent audits, and ad hoc reports for material events

✅ The cardinal rule of treasury reporting is "no surprises" -- the governance community should never learn about material events from external sources first

✅ MakerDAO's four-phase evolution demonstrates the progression from ad hoc management to institutional-grade treasury operations, generating $50-100M+ in annual yield from RWA allocations

✅ Key governance lessons: start simple and scale, build consensus before deploying capital, professional management pays for itself, and diversify before you need to
