# 2.1 Reading a DAO's Financial Statements

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Identify the key line items on a DAO income statement and their data sources
- Distinguish between gross protocol revenue and net protocol revenue
- Construct a DAO balance sheet with appropriate treatment of native token holdings
- Calculate DAO runway from cash flow analysis
- Apply liquidity discounts to native token holdings
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Key Terms**

- **Gross Protocol Revenue**: Total fees generated by the protocol's economic activity
- **Net Protocol Revenue**: Fees actually accruing to the DAO treasury or token holders (often much less than gross)
- **Token-Based Compensation Expense**: The dilutive cost of paying contributors in native tokens — analogous to stock-based compensation (ASC 718)
- **Protocol-Controlled Value (PCV)**: Assets deployed in DeFi protocols (LP positions, lending positions, yield farming)
- **Liquidity Discount**: A reduction applied to asset values reflecting the market impact of selling large positions
- **Treasury Paradox**: When a DAO is wealthy in notional token value but cash-poor in deployable capital
- **Runway**: The number of months a DAO can sustain operations from its liquid treasury before running out of funds
{% endhint %}

## The DAO Income Statement

A DAO's income statement captures the protocol's revenue generation and expense structure over a defined period. While specific line items vary by DAO type, the general structure is:

### Revenue

**Protocol Revenue** refers to fees generated by the protocol's core function:

| DAO Type | Revenue Source | Example |
|----------|---------------|---------|
| DEX | Trading fees | Uniswap: fees on each swap |
| Lending | Interest rate spread | Aave: spread between borrow and lend rates |
| Liquid Staking | Commission on staking rewards | Lido: 10% of staking rewards |
| Stablecoin Issuer | Stability fees + liquidation penalties | MakerDAO: interest on borrowed DAI |

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Critical Distinction**

There is a crucial difference between **gross** protocol revenue and **net** protocol revenue.

**Example:** Uniswap generates over $500M in annualized gross protocol revenue. But the DAO treasury captures **none of it** — all fees go to liquidity providers. The "fee switch" (if activated) would redirect a portion to the treasury.

If you confuse gross and net protocol revenue, your valuation will be fundamentally wrong. Always ask: *"Who actually receives these fees?"*
{% endhint %}

**Other Revenue** includes:
- Treasury yield (interest on stablecoins, staking rewards on ETH)
- Investment gains (appreciation of treasury assets)
- Grant revenue (income from ecosystem funds or other DAOs)
- Service revenue (consulting, integration support)

### Expenses

**Contributor Compensation** is typically the largest expense. DAOs pay core contributors through combinations of stablecoins, native tokens (often with vesting schedules), and performance bonuses.

**Grant Expenses** represent funds distributed to ecosystem participants for development, community building, and marketing — analogous to R&D and marketing expenses in traditional companies.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Example: Optimism RetroPGF**

Optimism's Retroactive Public Goods Funding (RetroPGF) program has distributed hundreds of millions of dollars to ecosystem contributors. This is a massive "grant expense" line item that directly affects the DAO's financial sustainability.
{% endhint %}

**Infrastructure Costs** include gas fees, oracle costs, hosting for off-chain components, security audits, and legal expenses. Generally modest relative to compensation but can spike during network congestion.

**Token-Based Compensation Expense** — When DAOs pay contributors in native tokens, this is both:
- A real economic cost (dilution to existing holders)
- A non-cash expense (no treasury stablecoins leave)

{% hint style="info" %}
**Formula**

**Token compensation accounting** requires tracking:

`Fair value at grant date × Number of tokens granted = Total compensation expense`

This expense is recognized over the vesting period, analogous to ASC 718 stock-based compensation accounting — but without the standardized framework.
{% endhint %}

## The DAO Balance Sheet

A DAO's balance sheet captures the protocol's assets, liabilities, and net position at a point in time.

### Assets

**Liquid Assets**: Stablecoins (USDC, USDT, DAI), ETH, and other immediately liquid tokens. Analogous to cash and cash equivalents.

**Native Token Holdings**: The DAO's unallocated governance tokens held in the treasury.

{% hint style="danger" %}
**The Great Debate: Are Native Tokens an Asset?**

This is one of the most significant accounting questions in DAO finance.

**Arguments FOR inclusion:**
- The DAO controls these tokens and can sell them to raise capital

**Arguments AGAINST inclusion:**
- Selling native tokens dilutes existing holders and depresses price
- The "asset" is partially self-referential — its value depends on the market's belief that it *won't* be sold

**Best Practice:** Present balance sheets **both** including and excluding native token holdings. When including them, apply a significant **liquidity discount** (typically 30-70%) reflecting market impact of potential sales.
{% endhint %}

**Protocol-Controlled Value (PCV)**: Assets deployed in DeFi protocols — LP positions, lending positions, yield farming strategies. Analogous to investments on a traditional balance sheet. Must be valued at fair market value with appropriate risk adjustments.

**Intangible Assets**: The protocol's codebase, brand, user base, network effects, and partnerships. Not recorded on any formal balance sheet, but they represent the majority of most DAOs' economic value — just as intangible assets dominate the value of tech companies.

### Liabilities

| Type | Description | Traditional Analog |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Vesting Obligations** | Tokens committed to contributors/investors not yet distributed | Stock compensation obligations |
| **Outstanding Debt** | Protocol bonds, credit lines, or formal borrowing | Corporate debt |
| **Operational Commitments** | Multi-year contributor agreements, committed grant programs | Operating leases, purchase obligations |
| **Contingent Liabilities** | Potential losses from exploits, regulatory actions, litigation | Legal contingencies |

## The DAO Cash Flow Statement

Cash flow analysis for DAOs focuses on three categories analogous to the traditional statement of cash flows:

**Operating Cash Flows** — the protocol's core economic engine: fee revenue minus operational expenses, denominated in stablecoins or ETH.

{% hint style="success" %}
**Key Point**: A DAO with positive operating cash flow is self-sustaining. One with negative operating cash flow is depleting its treasury and has a finite runway.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="info" %}
**Formula**

**DAO Runway Calculation:**

`Runway (months) = Liquid Treasury Assets (stablecoins + liquid crypto) / Monthly Net Operating Cash Outflow`

This is the **single most important metric** for assessing a DAO's financial health:
- **< 12 months:** Existential pressure — the DAO must cut expenses or raise capital
- **12-24 months:** Caution zone — strategic flexibility is limited
- **24-36 months:** Healthy — room for investment and experimentation
- **36+ months:** Strong — full strategic flexibility
{% endhint %}

**Investment Cash Flows** — treasury deployment into yield-generating positions, acquisitions of other protocols or assets, and strategic investments.

**Financing Cash Flows** — token sales from treasury, debt issuance, and other external capital raising. For most DAOs, significant positive financing flows come from **OTC token sales** — negotiated sales of treasury tokens to institutional investors at a discount to market price.

For a worked example of cash flow analysis, see the [Uniswap Case Study](case-study-uniswap.md).

## Section Summary

- DAO financial statements capture the same economic realities as traditional statements — revenue, expenses, assets, liabilities, cash flows — but must be constructed from disparate on-chain and off-chain sources
- Always distinguish between gross and net protocol revenue
- Native token holdings on the balance sheet require careful treatment — present with and without, apply liquidity discounts
- Runway is the single most important metric for DAO financial health
- The analyst's ability to synthesize multiple data sources into a coherent financial picture is a core professional competency
