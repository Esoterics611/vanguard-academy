# 1.4 Professional Standards and Ethics of DAO Investment Banking

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Apply CFA ethical standards to the decentralized finance context
- Explain how fiduciary duty operates when the client is a DAO
- Identify the key AML/KYC compliance obligations for DAO investment bankers
- Recognize and navigate ethical dilemmas unique to token-based advisory
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Key Terms**

- **Fiduciary Duty**: The legal and ethical obligation to act in the best interest of clients, placing their interests above your own
- **MNPI (Material Non-Public Information)**: Information that would affect an investment decision and is not yet publicly available
- **FATF**: Financial Action Task Force — the intergovernmental body that sets global AML standards
- **VASP (Virtual Asset Service Provider)**: Any entity that facilitates virtual asset transactions — a classification that may apply to DAO investment bankers
- **AML/KYC**: Anti-Money Laundering / Know Your Customer — regulatory frameworks requiring identity verification and transaction monitoring
- **Chinese Wall**: An information barrier within a firm preventing conflicts of interest between advisory and trading functions
{% endhint %}

## 1.4.1 Adapting CFA Ethical Standards to Decentralized Finance

The CFA Institute's Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct provides the gold standard for ethical behavior in investment management. These standards must be **adapted — not abandoned** — for the decentralized context.

### Standard I: Professionalism — Integrity of Capital Markets

In traditional finance, this standard prohibits insider trading and market manipulation. In the DAO context, MNPI exists but operates differently.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Example: MNPI in DAOs**

A DAO contributor with advance knowledge of a governance proposal that will significantly affect token price has information comparable to corporate insider knowledge. Imagine knowing, before public announcement, that:
- A major protocol will activate its fee switch (potentially doubling token value)
- A treasury diversification will sell 10% of native tokens (creating sell pressure)
- A merger with another protocol is being negotiated

**Best practice:** Any person with access to pre-publication governance proposals, treasury decisions, or strategic initiatives should be restricted from trading the protocol's token until the information becomes public.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Common Mistake**: Assuming that because DeFi is "decentralized," insider trading rules don't apply. They do — both ethically and increasingly legally. The SEC has brought enforcement actions based on insider knowledge of token listings and protocol changes.
{% endhint %}

Market manipulation in DeFi takes forms not seen in traditional markets:
- **Wash trading** on DEXs to inflate volume metrics
- **Governance attacks** using flash-loaned tokens
- **Coordinated pump-and-dump schemes** in low-liquidity token markets

The DAO investment banker must not participate in or facilitate any such activities.

### Standard III: Duties to Clients

When your client is a DAO, "duties to clients" means duties to the **entire token holder community** — not just the delegates or core team members you interact with directly.

{% hint style="success" %}
**Key Point**: Your analysis and recommendations must serve the protocol's long-term interests, even when that conflicts with the preferences of the core team or large token holders who originated the engagement. This is a higher standard of care than traditional IB, where you serve the corporation's board.
{% endhint %}

Confidentiality is more nuanced in the DAO context. While certain information (security vulnerabilities, pre-publication partnership details) must remain confidential, the general expectation is transparency. The DAO investment banker must clearly delineate what requires confidentiality and what should be disclosed to the community.

### Standard V: Investment Analysis and Recommendations

Diligence and reasonable basis are paramount. The DAO investment banker must have a thorough understanding of:
- The protocol's smart contract architecture
- Governance mechanisms
- Tokenomics
- Competitive landscape
- Risk factors

...before issuing any recommendation.

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Common Mistake**: "Move fast and break things" is not an acceptable standard when advising on decisions that affect the financial interests of thousands of token holders. The tendency in crypto culture toward promotional language and hyperbolic claims is antithetical to professional investment banking standards.
{% endhint %}

## 1.4.2 Fiduciary Duty in a Decentralized Context

Fiduciary duty raises novel questions in the DAO context:

**To whom is the duty owed?**

In traditional advisory, the duty is owed to the corporation (not individual shareholders). For DAOs, the duty is owed to the protocol and its community of stakeholders — token holders, users, contributors, and the broader ecosystem that depends on the protocol's continued operation.

**What happens when stakeholder interests conflict?**

| Stakeholder | Typical Preference | Potential Conflict |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Token holders | Short-term price appreciation | May favor aggressive buybacks over treasury conservation |
| Users | Lower fees | Conflicts with higher revenue for token holders |
| Contributors | Stable compensation | Competes with treasury conservation |
| Ecosystem partners | Protocol stability | May resist needed but disruptive changes |

The DAO investment banker must navigate these tensions transparently, presenting analysis that acknowledges trade-offs rather than advocating for one constituency over others.

**How is fiduciary duty enforced?**

In traditional finance: regulatory sanctions, civil liability, criminal prosecution. In the DAO context, enforcement is still developing. **Reputation** — on-chain and off-chain — serves as the primary enforcement mechanism. The DAO investment banker's track record of honest, competent service is their most valuable asset.

## 1.4.3 Anti-Money Laundering and Regulatory Compliance

DAO investment banking does not operate in a regulatory vacuum.

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Critical Warning**: The decentralized ethos of crypto should not be confused with a license to ignore the law. AML, KYC, and sanctions compliance obligations apply to anyone providing financial services, regardless of whether those services are delivered in a traditional or decentralized context.
{% endhint %}

The FATF has issued guidance clarifying that certain DAO activities may trigger obligations under the FATF Recommendations. If a DAO investment banker facilitates token sales, treasury management, or other VASP activities, compliance with local AML regulations is required.

**Best practice for DAO investment banking firms:**

1. Establish AML/KYC procedures appropriate to your operating jurisdictions
2. Screen counterparties against OFAC and other sanctions lists
3. Maintain transaction records sufficient to demonstrate compliance
4. Refuse engagements with DAOs or individuals that present unacceptable AML risk
5. Engage qualified legal counsel for ongoing regulatory compliance

{% hint style="info" %}
**Landmark Case: CFTC v. Ooki DAO (2022)**

The CFTC's enforcement action against Ooki DAO established that DAOs can be held liable as unincorporated associations. This precedent means that DAO governance participants — including advisors — may face personal liability for regulatory violations. Every DAO advisory engagement must account for this reality.
{% endhint %}

## Section Summary

- CFA ethical standards apply to DAO IB — adapted, not abandoned
- MNPI exists in DAOs (pre-publication governance proposals, strategic plans) and trading restrictions should apply
- Fiduciary duty is owed to the protocol and its entire community, not just the core team
- Stakeholder conflicts are inevitable; the banker presents transparent trade-off analysis
- AML/KYC compliance is mandatory, not optional, for professional DAO investment bankers
- Reputation is the primary enforcement mechanism for professional standards in this space
