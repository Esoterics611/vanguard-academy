## 1.1 Introduction: Why This Field Must Exist

The global investment banking industry generates approximately $100 billion in annual revenue across advisory, underwriting, trading, and asset management functions. These services exist because complex economic organizations require sophisticated financial intermediation to allocate capital efficiently, execute transformative transactions, and navigate competitive markets.

DAOs are complex economic organizations. As of 2024, DAO treasuries collectively manage over **$25 billion in assets**. The largest DAOs — Uniswap, Lido, Arbitrum, Optimism, MakerDAO (now Sky) — operate protocol businesses generating hundreds of millions of dollars in annualized revenue. They employ contributors, ship products, compete for market share, acquire other protocols, raise capital, and make strategic decisions about resource allocation.

Yet no formalized discipline of investment banking exists to serve them.

{% hint style="danger" %}
**The Problem**

DAOs today make billion-dollar decisions — treasury diversification, protocol mergers, token launches, strategic pivots — using ad hoc processes, informal governance proposals, and community sentiment rather than rigorous financial analysis. The consequences are predictable: value destruction through poorly structured token launches, failed mergers due to inadequate due diligence, treasury mismanagement that depletes protocol runways, and strategic drift born of absent competitive analysis.
{% endhint %}

This handbook exists to close that gap. It establishes the intellectual foundations, analytical frameworks, and practical methodologies for a new professional discipline: investment banking for decentralized organizations.

{% hint style="success" %}
**Key Point**: The students of this course will become the first practitioners of a discipline that every serious DAO will eventually require. There is an enormous first-mover advantage in defining a field before others recognize it needs to exist.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Think About It**

If DAOs manage $25 billion+ in collective assets and make consequential financial decisions daily, why hasn't a formal IB discipline emerged for them yet? What barriers — cultural, technical, regulatory — have prevented it? And what has changed to make it possible now?
{% endhint %}
