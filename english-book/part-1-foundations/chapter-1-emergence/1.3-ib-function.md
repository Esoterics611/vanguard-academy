# 1.3 The Investment Banking Function: Traditional to Decentralized

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Describe the six core functions of traditional investment banking
- Map each traditional function to its DAO equivalent
- Explain how the advisory engagement model changes when your client is a DAO
- Identify the key differences in compensation, conflict management, and deliverable format
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Key Terms**

- **Advisory Services**: Counsel on strategic transactions (M&A, divestitures, restructurings)
- **Underwriting**: Facilitating issuance of securities (equity or debt) to raise capital
- **TGE (Token Generation Event)**: The DAO equivalent of an IPO — the initial distribution of a protocol's token
- **Market Making**: Providing liquidity by posting bid and ask prices for a security or token
- **Fairness Opinion**: An independent assessment that a proposed transaction price is fair to shareholders/token holders
- **Engagement Letter**: The formal agreement between an investment bank and its client defining scope, fees, and terms
{% endhint %}

## 1.3.1 Core Functions of Traditional Investment Banking

To build a framework for decentralized investment banking, we must first understand what traditional investment banks actually do. The industry performs **six core functions**:

| Function | What It Does | How Fees Work |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Advisory** | M&A, divestitures, restructurings, fairness opinions | 1-3% of deal value + retainer |
| **Underwriting** | IPOs, secondary offerings, bond issuances | 3-7% (equity) / 0.5-2% (debt) |
| **Sales & Trading** | Market-making, block trading, liquidity provision | Bid-ask spreads, commissions |
| **Research** | Equity research, credit analysis, industry reports | Supports advisory & trading clients |
| **Asset Management** | Managing portfolios for institutions and HNW clients | 1-2% AUM + performance fees |
| **Restructuring** | Distressed debt, Chapter 11, turnaround advisory | Retainer + success fees |

## 1.3.2 Mapping Traditional Functions to the DAO Context

Each traditional function has a direct analog in the DAO ecosystem. This mapping establishes the framework for the entire handbook.

{% tabs %}
{% tab title="Traditional IB" %}
1. **Advisory Services** — Advise CEO and board on M&A, strategy
2. **Underwriting** — Manage IPOs and bond issuances
3. **Sales & Trading** — Provide institutional liquidity
4. **Research** — Publish equity/credit research
5. **Asset Management** — Manage client portfolios
6. **Restructuring** — Navigate financial distress
{% endtab %}

{% tab title="DAO Equivalent" %}
1. **DAO Strategic Advisory** — Advise governance community through proposals, delegate communication, and published analysis
2. **TGE Management** — Design tokenomics, manage launch, coordinate market makers, secure exchange listings
3. **DeFi Market Making** — Provide DEX liquidity, manage AMM positions, facilitate OTC token transactions
4. **Protocol Research & Due Diligence** — Analyze protocol mechanics, smart contract risk, competitive positioning
5. **DAO Treasury Management** — Asset allocation, risk management, yield optimization for the DAO treasury
6. **Protocol Restructuring** — Token holder coordination, emergency governance, treasury triage, strategic pivots
{% endtab %}
{% endtabs %}

{% hint style="success" %}
**Key Point**: The fundamental shift is in *who the client is*. In traditional IB, you advise a CEO and board — a small group of decision-makers. In DAO IB, you advise a governance community — a distributed set of token holders. You must persuade a community, not merely advise a C-suite.
{% endhint %}

## 1.3.3 The DAO Investment Banking Engagement Model

Traditional engagements follow a clear path: company retains bank via engagement letter, bank performs analysis, bank is compensated through deal-linked fees. For DAOs, every step must be adapted.

### Mandate Origination

Rather than a CEO calling a banker, DAO advisory mandates originate through **governance proposals**. A proposal requesting advisory services must be submitted, debated, and approved by token holders. This means the investment banker must be skilled not only in financial analysis but in **governance communication** — framing the value proposition of advisory services in a way that persuades a distributed stakeholder base.

### Compensation Structures

| Structure | Description | Pros | Cons |
|-----------|-------------|------|------|
| **Fixed token grants** | Treasury allocates native tokens | Aligns advisor with protocol | Exposes advisor to token price risk |
| **Stablecoin success fees** | USDC/DAI paid on deal completion | Mirrors traditional fee structure | Less alignment with long-term protocol health |
| **Streaming payments** | Ongoing compensation via Sablier/Superfluid | Suits retained advisory | Complex to set up and manage |
| **Hybrid** | Stable base + token upside | Best incentive alignment | Most complex to negotiate |

### Conflict of Interest Management

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Common Mistake**: Assuming that on-chain transparency eliminates conflict of interest problems. While all token holdings are publicly visible on-chain, conflicts are actually *more pervasive* in DAO IB — advisors almost always hold governance tokens in client DAOs. Best practice requires: full disclosure of holdings, recusal from governance votes on advised matters, and clear separation between advisory recommendations and personal positions.
{% endhint %}

### Deliverable Format

Traditional IB deliverables — pitch books, fairness opinions, information memoranda — are confidential documents shared with a limited audience. In the DAO context, much advisory work product must be **public or semi-public** to support governance decision-making.

This creates both challenges (competitors can see your analysis) and opportunities (transparent, high-quality analysis builds reputation and attracts future mandates).

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Think About It**

If your advisory analysis must be public to support governance voting, how do you maintain competitive advantage as a DAO investment bank? What becomes the moat — proprietary data, analytical quality, relationship capital, or something else?
{% endhint %}

## Section Summary

- Traditional IB has six core functions; each maps directly to a DAO equivalent
- The fundamental difference is the client: distributed governance community vs. corporate C-suite
- Engagement mandates originate through governance proposals, not phone calls
- Compensation can be token-based, stablecoin-based, streaming, or hybrid
- Conflicts of interest are more transparent but also more pervasive in the DAO context
- Advisory deliverables are often public, changing the competitive dynamics
