# Chapter 20: Go-to-Market Strategy and Advisory Delivery

Go-to-market strategy defines how a protocol reaches its target users, communicates its value proposition, and converts awareness into active adoption. Advisory delivery is the operational discipline that transforms strategic analysis into governance-ready recommendations and shepherds them through implementation. Together, they represent the final -- and most practical -- competencies of the DAO investment banker: the ability to craft a protocol's market approach and deliver actionable strategic guidance to decentralized governance communities.

## Chapter Overview

This chapter covers the complete GTM and advisory delivery toolkit:

- **The Protocol GTM Canvas** -- an eight-component framework for defining target users, value propositions, discovery channels, conversion mechanisms, retention strategies, growth metrics, competitive response, and milestone timelines
- **Multi-chain deployment GTM** -- chain selection criteria, deployment prioritization by ROI, and localization strategies for geographically concentrated ecosystems
- **Product launch GTM** -- a four-phase playbook from beta through scaling
- **The Strategic Advisory Engagement Model** -- a five-phase process from discovery through implementation support, with defined deliverables at each stage
- **Common advisory engagement types** -- competitive response, product strategy, fee optimization, governance restructuring, and crisis advisory
- **The Strategic Pitch Book** -- structure, content, and best practices for the advisor's primary deliverable

## Key Concepts

{% hint style="info" %}
**GTM as a Living Document**

Protocols should revisit their go-to-market strategy at every major inflection point: new chain deployments, major product upgrades, competitive threats, and strategic pivots. A GTM plan is not a one-time exercise -- it is a strategic framework that evolves with the protocol's competitive environment and growth trajectory.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Advisory Delivery Requires Governance Fluency**

Unlike traditional consulting where recommendations are delivered to a CEO or board, DAO strategic advisory requires presenting to an entire governance community -- delegates, token holders, core contributors, and community members. The advisor must translate complex analysis into governance proposals that can survive public scrutiny, forum debate, and on-chain voting. This fundamentally changes both the format and the process of advisory delivery.
{% endhint %}
