# 18.3 Building the Competitive Landscape Map

The Five Forces analysis provides a macro-level understanding of competitive dynamics within a protocol category. The next step is to build a **protocol-level competitive landscape map** -- a structured, quantitative assessment of how individual protocols compare across the dimensions that drive competitive success.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Apply the Protocol Competitive Matrix across 10 dimensions with 1-5 scoring
- Construct radar charts to visualize competitive positioning
- Identify competitive gaps and strategic opportunities from matrix analysis
- Conduct a complete sector-level competitive landscape assessment
{% endhint %}

---

## 18.3.1 The Protocol Competitive Matrix

The Protocol Competitive Matrix evaluates each protocol across **10 dimensions**, each scored from 1 (weakest) to 5 (strongest). This structured framework ensures consistent, comparable analysis across competitors and reduces the risk of overlooking critical competitive factors.

### The 10 Dimensions

| # | Dimension | What It Measures | Scoring Criteria |
|---|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| 1 | **Market Position** | Market share, share trend, absolute scale | 5 = dominant share (>40%), growing; 1 = marginal share, declining |
| 2 | **Product Quality** | Technical sophistication, UX, feature set, innovation pipeline | 5 = best-in-class, continuous innovation; 1 = outdated, no roadmap |
| 3 | **Liquidity & Capital Efficiency** | Depth of liquidity, capital utilization rate, slippage | 5 = deepest liquidity, best capital efficiency; 1 = shallow, inefficient |
| 4 | **Security Track Record** | Audit history, exploit history, time in production, bug bounty program | 5 = clean record, extensive audits, 3+ years; 1 = major exploit, unaudited |
| 5 | **Governance Health** | Voter participation, proposal quality, delegate diversity, governance agility | 5 = active, diverse, responsive; 1 = concentrated, inactive, dysfunctional |
| 6 | **Financial Strength** | Treasury size (non-native), revenue sustainability, burn rate | 5 = substantial treasury, profitable, low burn; 1 = minimal treasury, unprofitable |
| 7 | **Ecosystem Integration** | Number and depth of integrations, composability position | 5 = deeply integrated, infrastructure-level; 1 = isolated, few integrations |
| 8 | **Team Quality** | Developer talent, track record, retention, depth of contributors | 5 = top-tier team, deep bench, strong retention; 1 = small team, high turnover |
| 9 | **Regulatory Positioning** | Compliance readiness, legal structure, geographic risk management | 5 = well-structured, compliance-ready; 1 = no legal entity, regulatory risk |
| 10 | **Community & Brand** | Brand recognition, community engagement, developer mindshare | 5 = iconic brand, vibrant community; 1 = unknown, disengaged community |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Scoring Discipline**

Resist the temptation to cluster scores in the 3-4 range. Meaningful competitive differentiation requires honest assessment, including scores of 1-2 where warranted. A matrix where every protocol scores 3-4 across all dimensions provides no strategic insight. The value of the framework comes from identifying **where protocols meaningfully differ**.
{% endhint %}

### Scoring Template

Use this template for each protocol in the competitive set:

```
Protocol: _______________
Category: _______________
Date:     _______________

Dimension                    Score (1-5)    Evidence/Rationale
──────────────────────────   ───────────    ─────────────────────
1. Market Position           [ ]
2. Product Quality           [ ]
3. Liquidity & Cap. Eff.     [ ]
4. Security Track Record     [ ]
5. Governance Health         [ ]
6. Financial Strength        [ ]
7. Ecosystem Integration     [ ]
8. Team Quality              [ ]
9. Regulatory Positioning    [ ]
10. Community & Brand        [ ]

```

{% hint style="info" %}
**Evidence-Based Scoring**

Every score must be supported by specific, verifiable evidence. "I think Aave has good governance" is not sufficient. "Aave averages 15+ governance proposals per quarter with 30%+ delegate participation and has successfully executed complex parameter changes within 7 days" is evidence-based scoring. The evidence column is what transforms subjective opinion into defensible analysis.
{% endhint %}

## 18.3.2 Radar Chart Visualization

The Protocol Competitive Matrix is most effectively communicated through **radar charts** (also called spider diagrams). Each protocol is plotted as a polygon across the 10 dimensions, enabling immediate visual comparison.

### How to Read the Radar Chart

```
                    Market Position (1)
                         5
                         |
    Community (10)   4   |   2  Product Quality (2)
                  \  3   |  /
                   \ 2   | /
    Regulatory (9)--1----+----1-- Liquidity (3)
                   / 2   | \
                  /  3   |  \
    Team (8)     4   |   2  Security (4)
                         |
                    Integration (7)
                         |
              Fin. Strength (6)
                         |
                    Governance (5)
```

### Visualization Best Practices

| Practice | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **Limit to 4-5 protocols per chart** | More than 5 overlapping polygons becomes unreadable |
| **Use distinct colors** | Each protocol should be immediately distinguishable |
| **Include the scoring table alongside** | Radar charts show shape; tables show precise values |
| **Create category-specific charts** | Compare lending protocols against lending protocols, not against DEXs |
| **Highlight the focus protocol** | Use a bolder line or fill for the protocol you are advising |

{% hint style="success" %}
**Strategic Interpretation**

The radar chart reveals competitive positioning at a glance:

- **Larger polygon = stronger overall position** -- The protocol with the largest area has the strongest composite competitive position
- **Shape irregularities = competitive gaps** -- A deep indent on one dimension reveals a vulnerability competitors can exploit
- **Shape overlap = direct competition** -- Protocols with similar shapes are competing on the same dimensions and will likely face head-to-head rivalry
- **Unique protrusions = differentiation** -- A dimension where one protocol significantly outscores others represents a potential strategic advantage to leverage
{% endhint %}

## 18.3.3 From Matrix to Strategic Insight

The competitive matrix and radar chart are analytical tools, not strategic outputs. The strategic advisor must translate the quantitative assessment into actionable recommendations.

### Step 1: Identify Competitive Gaps

Compare the focus protocol's scores against the category leader:

```
Gap Analysis: [Focus Protocol] vs. [Category Leader]

Dimension                    Focus    Leader    Gap    Priority
──────────────────────────   ─────    ──────    ───    ────────
1. Market Position            3        5        -2     Monitor
2. Product Quality            4        4         0     Maintain
3. Liquidity & Cap. Eff.      2        5        -3     CRITICAL
4. Security Track Record      4        5        -1     Invest
5. Governance Health          3        4        -1     Improve
6. Financial Strength         3        4        -1     Improve
7. Ecosystem Integration      2        4        -2     STRATEGIC
8. Team Quality               4        4         0     Maintain
9. Regulatory Positioning     3        3         0     Maintain
10. Community & Brand         2        5        -3     CRITICAL
```

### Step 2: Classify Gaps by Strategic Priority

| Priority | Criteria | Action |
|----------|----------|--------|
| **CRITICAL** | Gap of 3+ on a high-weight dimension | Immediate strategic initiative required |
| **STRATEGIC** | Gap of 2 on a dimension with compounding effects (integration, liquidity) | Medium-term investment plan |
| **IMPROVE** | Gap of 1 on dimensions where improvement is achievable | Operational improvement |
| **MAINTAIN** | No gap or gap on low-weight dimensions | Continue current approach |
| **MONITOR** | Gap driven by market dynamics rather than protocol actions | Track metrics, intervene if gap widens |

### Step 3: Develop Strategic Recommendations

For each CRITICAL and STRATEGIC gap, the advisor should develop a specific recommendation:

{% hint style="success" %}
**Recommendation Format**

Each strategic recommendation should follow this structure:

1. **Gap identified** -- What dimension, what is the gap, why does it matter?
2. **Root cause** -- Why does this gap exist? Is it resource-constrained, strategic-choice-driven, or structural?
3. **Recommended action** -- Specific, actionable initiative with clear deliverables
4. **Resource requirement** -- What treasury allocation, team effort, or governance action is needed?
5. **Expected impact** -- How will this close the gap? What score improvement is targeted?
6. **Timeline** -- When should governance expect to see results?
7. **Monitoring metrics** -- How will progress be tracked?
{% endhint %}

## 18.3.4 Sector-Level Analysis

The Protocol Competitive Matrix is most powerful when applied across an entire sector. By scoring all significant competitors in a category, the strategic advisor can identify:

- **Category leaders and challengers** -- Who dominates, who is gaining ground
- **Strategic groups** -- Clusters of protocols competing on similar dimensions
- **Market gaps** -- Underserved segments where competitive pressure is low
- **Consolidation candidates** -- Weaker protocols that may be M&A targets
- **Emerging threats** -- New entrants with strong scores on innovation-related dimensions

{% hint style="info" %}
**Sector Analysis Deliverable**

A complete sector-level competitive analysis should include:

1. **Category definition** -- What protocols are included and why
2. **Competitive matrix** -- All protocols scored across 10 dimensions
3. **Radar chart comparison** -- Visual positioning of top 4-5 competitors
4. **Key competitive insights** -- 3-5 strategic observations about the sector
5. **Implications for the focus protocol** -- What the landscape means for strategic decisions
{% endhint %}

For a complete worked example of a sector-level competitive analysis, see the [Lending Protocol Sector Case Study](case-study-lending-sector.md).

## Key Takeaways

✅ The Protocol Competitive Matrix provides a structured, 10-dimension framework for comparing protocols: Market Position, Product Quality, Liquidity, Security, Governance, Financial Strength, Integration, Team, Regulatory, and Community

✅ Every score must be evidence-based -- the value of the framework depends on honest, verifiable assessments rather than subjective impressions

✅ Radar charts enable immediate visual comparison of competitive positioning, revealing gaps (indents), differentiation (protrusions), and direct rivalry (overlapping shapes)

✅ The strategic advisor must translate matrix scores into prioritized gap analysis and actionable recommendations with specific initiatives, resource requirements, and monitoring metrics

✅ Sector-level analysis -- scoring all significant competitors in a category -- reveals strategic groups, market gaps, consolidation candidates, and emerging threats
