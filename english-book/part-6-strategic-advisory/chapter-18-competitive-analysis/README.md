# Chapter 18: Competitive Analysis for Protocols

Strategic advisory is the pinnacle of the DAO investment banking function -- the discipline where financial analysis, competitive intelligence, and market judgment converge to shape a protocol's strategic direction. Unlike valuation (which is analytical) or transaction execution (which is procedural), competitive analysis demands synthesis: transforming publicly available on-chain data into actionable recommendations that a distributed governance community can understand, evaluate, and vote to adopt.

## Chapter Overview

This chapter builds the complete competitive analysis toolkit for the DAO investment banker:

- **The strategic advisory mandate** -- what strategic advisory is, why it is the highest-value function, and how competitive analysis in DeFi differs fundamentally from traditional industries
- **Porter's Five Forces adapted for DeFi** -- a systematic framework for analyzing competitive dynamics across protocol categories, from threat of new entrants to competitive rivalry
- **Building the competitive landscape map** -- a structured 10-dimension scoring framework for evaluating protocols against their competitors
- **Lending sector case study** -- a comprehensive competitive analysis of the lending protocol sector, demonstrating the framework in practice

## Key Concepts

{% hint style="info" %}
**DeFi Competitive Dynamics**

Competitive analysis in DeFi operates under fundamentally different rules than traditional industries. Open-source transparency means the analyst is *synthesis-constrained*, not *information-constrained*. Forkability eliminates traditional moats. Composability creates structural advantages that cannot be replicated by forking code alone. And the speed of competitive disruption compresses years of industry evolution into months.
{% endhint %}

{% hint style="warning" %}
**The Forkability Paradox**

Any protocol's code can be forked and redeployed within days -- but the factors that truly drive competitive advantage (liquidity depth, network effects, brand trust, integration density, governance community strength) cannot be forked. The strategic advisor must distinguish between what is replicable and what is defensible.
{% endhint %}
