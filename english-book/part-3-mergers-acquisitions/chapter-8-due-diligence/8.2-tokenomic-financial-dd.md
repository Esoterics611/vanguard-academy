# 8.2 Tokenomic and Financial Due Diligence

Tokenomic due diligence evaluates the target's token design, supply dynamics, distribution, and economic sustainability — factors that directly affect both the transaction valuation and the post-merger token structure. Financial due diligence adapts traditional financial review to the protocol context.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Analyze token supply trajectories, unlock schedules, and holder distribution
- Evaluate value accrual mechanisms and their sustainability
- Assess token compatibility for merger execution
- Conduct revenue quality, treasury, and burn rate analysis for DAO targets
{% endhint %}

---

## 8.2 Workstream 2: Tokenomic Due Diligence

### 8.2.1 Token Supply Analysis

**Total Supply and Emission Schedule:** Map the complete token supply trajectory from genesis to maximum supply. Identify all emission sources (staking rewards, liquidity mining, contributor vesting, treasury grants) and all burn/deflation mechanisms. Project the token supply at 1, 2, 3, and 5 years.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Inflation Destroys Acquisition Value**

A protocol with aggressive token emission (high inflation) destroys value for existing holders. If the target inflates its supply by 20% annually through incentive programs, the acquirer is buying an asset that dilutes rapidly. The investment banker must model the impact of inflation on post-merger token economics.
{% endhint %}

**Circulating vs. Locked Supply:** What percentage of tokens is currently circulating? What is locked in vesting contracts, staking, governance locks, or team allocations? When do locked tokens unlock?

Token unlock schedules are critical for M&A timing. If a major unlock (e.g., founder/team tokens) occurs shortly after the merger, the selling pressure may depress the combined token's price.

**Token Holder Distribution:** Analyze distribution using on-chain data:

| Metric | What It Reveals |
|--------|----------------|
| % held by top 10 holders (excl. contracts) | Governance concentration |
| % held by top 100 holders | Breadth of meaningful participation |
| Gini coefficient of distribution | Overall equality of holdings |
| Whale wallet behavior | Accumulating vs. distributing patterns |

**Concentration risk:** If a small number of holders control a majority of governance tokens, they effectively control the protocol and can block or force the merger.

### 8.2.2 Value Accrual Mechanism Assessment

Evaluate how protocol revenue translates into token value (as covered in Part II):

- **Is the mechanism active or theoretical?** A protocol that has implemented fee distribution is more valuable than one where fee distribution is theoretically possible but not yet activated (the Uniswap fee switch problem).
- **Is the mechanism sustainable?** Fee distribution funded by token emissions rather than organic revenue is unsustainable. Distinguish between genuine value accrual and token-incentivized circularity.
- **What changes are under governance consideration?** Potential changes to fee structure, token burns, or distribution mechanisms affect forward-looking valuation.

### 8.2.3 Token Compatibility Assessment

When two DAOs merge, their tokens must be combined or exchanged:

| Compatibility Dimension | Key Question | Risk if Incompatible |
|------------------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **Token Standards** | Both ERC-20? Non-standard features? | Integration complexity |
| **Chain Deployment** | Same chain(s)? | Requires cross-chain bridge or new token |
| **Vesting Contracts** | Can vesting contracts distribute new token? | Manual conversion burden |
| **DeFi Integrations** | Target token used as collateral / LP? | Disruption to DeFi ecosystem |

If the target token is widely used as collateral in lending protocols, has deep liquidity pools on DEXs, or is integrated into other protocol mechanics, the transition to a new token must be carefully managed.

## 8.3 Workstream 3: Financial Due Diligence

### 8.3.1 Revenue Quality Assessment

**Revenue Decomposition:** Break down the target's revenue by source, chain, product, and counterparty. Identify top revenue contributors and assess concentration risk.

**Organic vs. Incentivized Revenue:** As discussed in Part II (Section 6.5.3), distinguish revenue driven by genuine user demand from revenue driven by token incentive programs. Calculate the **incentive-adjusted revenue** to assess sustainable economics.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Revenue Quality Spectrum**

```
High Quality ←————————————————————→ Low Quality
Organic fees    Recurring usage    Incentivized    One-time events
from loyal      from retained      activity that   (airdrops,
user base       users              stops when      token launches)
                                   rewards end
```

The investment banker must assess where the target's revenue falls on this spectrum.
{% endhint %}

**Revenue Persistence Analysis:** How sticky is the target's revenue?
- For a DEX: Is volume driven by organic swap demand or by MEV bots and arbitrageurs?
- For a lending protocol: How much borrowing demand is structural vs. cyclical?

**Revenue Trend Analysis:** Chart revenue on daily, weekly, and monthly bases for trailing 12 months. Identify trends, seasonality, and correlation with broader crypto market conditions.

### 8.3.2 Treasury Analysis

**Detailed Treasury Audit:** Verify all treasury holdings by wallet address:
1. List all known treasury wallet addresses
2. Verify each address on block explorers
3. Confirm multi-sig configurations and signers
4. Cross-reference governance proposals that authorized allocations
5. Identify discrepancies between reported and actual holdings

**Asset Quality Assessment:** Apply liquidity discounts to each asset category:

| Asset Category | Liquidity Discount | Rationale |
|---------------|-------------------|-----------|
| Stablecoins (USDC, USDT, DAI) | 0% | Fully liquid |
| Major crypto (ETH, BTC) | 0-5% | Highly liquid but volatile |
| Native governance tokens | 40-70% | Illiquid, self-referential value |
| LP positions | 5-15% | Impermanent loss + smart contract risk |
| Exotic DeFi positions | 20-50% | Complex risk profile |

**Commitment and Liability Register:** Document all outstanding commitments — approved grants, contributor agreements, operational commitments, and contingent liabilities (exploit reimbursements, legal claims).

```
Net Deployable Treasury = Liquid Assets - Outstanding Commitments - Operating Reserve (12 months)
```

This net figure represents the treasury capital actually available post-merger.

### 8.3.3 Expense and Burn Rate Analysis

**Contributor Cost Mapping:** Identify all contributors (core team, part-time, grant recipients) and their compensation. Calculate total monthly burn rate in stablecoin-equivalent terms.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Token-Based Compensation Risk**

Contributor compensation that is predominantly token-based creates uncertainty — if the token price declines, contributors may demand renegotiation or leave. This is a retention risk that directly affects post-merger integration.
{% endhint %}

**Infrastructure Cost Baseline:** Gas fees for on-chain operations, oracle subscriptions, hosting, monitoring, and third-party service contracts.

**Grant and Incentive Program Commitments:** Map all ongoing grant programs with remaining disbursement schedules. These represent committed expenses the acquirer inherits.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Token supply analysis must project inflation, unlock schedules, and concentration risk — all affect post-merger dynamics
- Value accrual mechanisms must be active and sustainable, not theoretical or emission-funded
- Token compatibility assessment prevents execution surprises during merger implementation
- Revenue quality assessment distinguishes organic, sustainable revenue from incentivized activity
- Treasury audit must verify wallet addresses, apply liquidity discounts, and calculate net deployable capital
- Burn rate analysis reveals the true cost structure the acquirer will inherit
