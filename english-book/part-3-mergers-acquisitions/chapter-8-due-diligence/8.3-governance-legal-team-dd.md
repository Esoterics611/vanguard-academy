# 8.3 Governance, Legal, and Team Due Diligence

Governance due diligence evaluates the target's decision-making processes, governance health, and the feasibility of governance integration post-merger. This workstream has no direct equivalent in traditional M&A and is uniquely critical in the DAO context. Legal and team due diligence round out the organizational assessment.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Assess governance mechanism design and its implications for M&A execution
- Evaluate governance health through participation metrics and conflict history
- Determine whether a merger can realistically achieve governance approval
- Identify legal and regulatory risks specific to DAO transactions
- Assess key person dependency and design contributor retention strategies
{% endhint %}

---

## 8.4 Workstream 4: Governance Due Diligence

### 8.4.1 Governance Mechanism Analysis

**Voting Mechanism:** What voting system does the target use?

| System | Description | M&A Implication |
|--------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Token-weighted** | One token = one vote | Plutocratic; large holders control outcome |
| **Quadratic voting** | Voting power = sqrt(tokens) | Broader participation; harder for whales to dominate |
| **Conviction voting** | Voting power increases with time held | Rewards long-term holders; slows decision-making |
| **Optimistic governance** | Proposals pass unless vetoed | Easier to pass mergers; easier to be exploited |
| **Hybrid** | Combination of mechanisms | Requires understanding each component |

**Quorum Requirements:** What percentage of total supply must participate for a vote to be valid?

{% hint style="warning" %}
**The Quorum Dilemma**

Low quorum (1-5%) makes governance susceptible to manipulation but makes it easier to pass merger proposals. High quorum (10-20%+) provides legitimate mandate but may make approval difficult to achieve. The investment banker must assess whether quorum is realistically achievable for a merger vote.
{% endhint %}

**Timelock and Execution Delays:** Standard timelocks range from 24 hours to 7 days. Longer timelocks provide safety but slow transaction execution.

**Delegation Structure:** Does governance support delegation? How concentrated is delegate power? Who are the top 10 delegates and what is their voting history?

**Multi-sig Authority:** Who are the signers? What is the threshold (e.g., 4-of-7)? Multi-sig signers represent concentrated trust and potential single points of failure.

### 8.4.2 Governance Health Assessment

**Participation Metrics:**
- Average voter turnout (as % of circulating supply) for last 10 proposals
- Trend in voter turnout (increasing, stable, declining)
- Number of unique voters per proposal
- Delegate participation rate

A DAO with declining governance participation is a DAO where the community is disengaging — a significant risk factor for post-merger integration.

**Governance Conflict History:**
- Contentious proposals (close vote margins, high community conflict)
- Governance attacks or manipulation attempts
- Factional dynamics within the governance community
- Relationship between core team and broader token holders

Governance conflicts are leading indicators of post-merger integration challenges. A target with fractious governance is more likely to resist merger implementation.

**Decision Quality Assessment:**
- Review outcomes of the last 20 governance decisions
- Were decisions well-reasoned and well-implemented?
- Did the DAO respond effectively to crises?
- Has governance produced strategic coherence or directionless drift?

### 8.4.3 Governance Feasibility Assessment

The practical question: **Can this transaction actually get approved?**

**Token Holder Mapping:** Identify the top 50 token holders and delegates. Classify each as:

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Likely supportive** | Aligned with merger rationale, will vote yes |
| **Likely opposed** | Structural or ideological reasons to vote no |
| **Persuadable/neutral** | Open to arguments; can be swayed |
| **Inactive** | Hold tokens but don't participate in governance |

For the merger to pass, supportive + persuadable categories must collectively exceed quorum and achieve the required threshold.

**Opposition Analysis:** Common sources of opposition:
- Core team members who fear losing autonomy
- Large holders who prefer the status quo
- Community members ideologically opposed to consolidation
- Competitors or their allies who benefit from target independence

{% hint style="success" %}
**Governance Campaign Strategy**

The investment banker should develop a governance campaign — the DAO equivalent of shareholder solicitation:
- **Stakeholder engagement plan** — which delegates to approach, in what order
- **Messaging framework** — how to present benefits to different constituencies
- **Q&A preparation** — anticipating objections and preparing responses
- **Timeline** — community discussion, temperature checks, and formal votes
{% endhint %}

## 8.5 Workstream 5: Legal and Regulatory Due Diligence

### 8.5.1 Legal Entity Assessment

**Legal Wrapper Analysis:** Does the target operate through a legal entity?

| Jurisdiction | Structure | Common Usage |
|-------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Wyoming** | DAO LLC | U.S.-based DAOs seeking legal recognition |
| **Marshall Islands** | DAO Act entity | Decentralized-first projects |
| **Cayman Islands** | Foundation Company | Crypto projects needing institutional structure |
| **Switzerland** | Association | European crypto entities |
| **Panama / BVI** | Various | Flexible offshore structures |

The legal wrapper affects transaction structure, tax implications, regulatory compliance, and liability allocation.

### 8.5.2 Regulatory Exposure Assessment

**Securities Classification Risk:** Is the target's token at risk of classification as a security (Howey test)? Tokens with strong economic rights (fee distribution, profit sharing) face higher classification risk than pure governance tokens. In an M&A context, the transaction itself may constitute an offering of securities.

**VASP/MSB Registration:** Does the target require registration as a Virtual Asset Service Provider or Money Services Business?

**Sanctions Compliance:** Has the target implemented sanctions screening? Has it processed transactions involving sanctioned addresses? The Tornado Cash sanctions (OFAC, August 2022) demonstrated that DeFi protocols face sanctions risk.

**Pending Legal Actions:** Lawsuits, regulatory investigations, or enforcement actions against the target, its foundation, or core team members.

### 8.5.3 Intellectual Property Assessment

**Open Source License Compliance:** Common licenses include MIT, GPL, Apache 2.0, and BSL (Business Source License). BSL-licensed code carries specific restrictions on commercial use.

**Proprietary Components:** Some teams develop proprietary off-chain components (MEV strategies, risk models, data analytics) that represent genuine IP.

**Brand and Trademark:** Does the target own registered trademarks? Trademark protection is often neglected by crypto projects.

## 8.6 Workstream 6: Team and Contributor Due Diligence

### 8.6.1 Key Person Identification

Identify all critical contributors:
- Core developers (smart contract, frontend, DevOps)
- Protocol researchers and mechanism designers
- Community managers and governance facilitators
- Business development and partnership leads
- Operations and finance personnel

For each key person, assess:
- Contribution to protocol development and operations
- Compensation structure and satisfaction level
- Likelihood of remaining post-merger
- Restrictive covenants or non-compete obligations
- Reputation and community standing

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Key Person Dependency Risk**

Protocols with high key person dependency are riskier acquisitions because the most valuable asset (human capital) can walk away. If the lead smart contract developer left, could the protocol continue to operate? If the community manager departed, would governance participation collapse?
{% endhint %}

### 8.6.2 Contributor Retention Strategy

The acquirer should develop a retention strategy as part of DD:

**Retention Package Components:**

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Signing bonus** | Immediate stablecoin payment at merger closing |
| **Enhanced token vesting** | New vesting grants in the merged entity's token |
| **Role guarantees** | Contractual commitments on role and responsibilities |
| **Performance bonuses** | Tied to integration milestones |

**Cost Estimation:** Total retention package cost must be included in the total acquisition price analysis.

**Cultural Compatibility Assessment:** Will the target's contributors integrate well? Differences in work culture, compensation expectations, governance philosophy, and technical standards can create friction that impairs integration.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Governance DD has no traditional equivalent and is uniquely critical for DAO M&A
- Token holder mapping and governance feasibility analysis predict whether the vote will pass
- A governance campaign strategy (stakeholder engagement, messaging, Q&A) is the DAO equivalent of shareholder solicitation
- Legal DD must assess entity structure, securities risk, sanctions compliance, and IP
- Key person dependency is the biggest retention risk — human capital can walk away
- Contributor retention costs are a real cost of the transaction and must be budgeted
