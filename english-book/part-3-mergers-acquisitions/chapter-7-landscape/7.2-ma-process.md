# 7.2 The M&A Process for DAOs: An Overview

The traditional M&A process follows a well-defined sequence: strategy development, target identification, preliminary valuation, approach and indication of interest, due diligence, definitive agreement negotiation, regulatory approval, closing, and post-merger integration. The DAO M&A process follows this same general sequence but with critical modifications at each stage.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Key Difference from Traditional M&A**

The governance approval phase is the most unpredictable and is where many DAO M&A transactions fail. Governance approval requires not only a favorable vote but genuine community buy-in — a narrow governance victory with vocal opposition can doom integration efforts.
{% endhint %}

## 7.2.1 The DAO M&A Process Timeline

### Phase 1 — Strategic Assessment and Mandate (Weeks 1-4)

The investment banker works with the DAO's core team, delegates, or a designated M&A committee to define the strategic rationale for a transaction. This phase produces a strategic assessment document that is either kept confidential (if approaching specific targets) or published as a governance discussion to build community support for M&A activity.

**Key Deliverable:** M&A Strategy Memo — outlining strategic rationale, target criteria, valuation parameters, and proposed transaction structure.

### Phase 2 — Target Identification and Screening (Weeks 3-8)

The investment banker identifies and evaluates potential targets based on strategic fit, financial profile, governance dynamics, and transaction feasibility. Unlike traditional M&A, where target information is often confidential, much of the information needed to evaluate DAO targets is publicly available on-chain and in governance forums.

**Key Deliverable:** Target Screening Matrix — detailed profiles of 5-15 potential targets ranked by strategic attractiveness and transaction feasibility.

### Phase 3 — Preliminary Valuation and Approach (Weeks 6-12)

The investment banker develops preliminary valuations for priority targets and structures an initial approach. In the DAO context, the "approach" may take three forms:

| Approach Type | Description | Best When |
|--------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Private** | Direct communication with target's core team or key delegates | Competitive dynamics require confidentiality |
| **Semi-Public** | Governance forum post proposing a strategic discussion | Community expects transparency in strategic initiatives |
| **Public** | Formal governance proposal to authorize M&A discussions | Target team is likely receptive; acquirer culture values openness |

**Key Deliverable:** Preliminary Valuation Report and Approach Strategy Memo.

### Phase 4 — Due Diligence (Weeks 10-20)

If the target is receptive, comprehensive due diligence is conducted across ten workstreams (covered in depth in Chapter 8). Unlike traditional M&A, where due diligence requires access to confidential data rooms, much of DAO due diligence can be conducted using publicly available on-chain data and governance records. However, certain aspects — key person assessments, undisclosed vulnerabilities, pipeline information — still require cooperative information sharing.

**Key Deliverable:** Due Diligence Report with findings and risk assessment.

### Phase 5 — Transaction Structuring and Negotiation (Weeks 16-28)

The investment banker structures the transaction — token exchange ratio, governance integration mechanism, treasury combination terms, contributor retention packages, and operational integration plan. Negotiation in the DAO context often involves simultaneous discussions with the target's core team, key delegates, and large token holders.

**Key Deliverable:** Term Sheet / Transaction Proposal Document.

### Phase 6 — Governance Approval (Weeks 24-36)

Both DAOs must approve the transaction through their respective governance processes. This typically involves:

1. Publication of a detailed governance proposal with full transaction terms
2. Community discussion period (typically 1-2 weeks)
3. Formal voting period (typically 3-7 days)
4. Quorum requirements must be met on both sides

{% hint style="danger" %}
**The Governance Gauntlet**

This phase is the most unpredictable and is where many DAO M&A transactions fail. A narrow governance victory with vocal opposition can doom integration efforts. The investment banker must develop a governance campaign strategy — the DAO equivalent of shareholder solicitation in a traditional proxy fight.
{% endhint %}

**Key Deliverable:** Governance Proposals (for both DAOs), Fairness Opinion (if requested), Community Q&A Documentation.

### Phase 7 — Execution and Closing (Weeks 32-44)

Technical implementation of the transaction: smart contract deployment for token swaps, treasury migration, governance contract updates, and any required legal entity restructuring.

**Key Deliverable:** Transaction Execution Plan, Smart Contract Audit Report, Legal Documentation.

### Phase 8 — Post-Merger Integration (Weeks 40-80+)

The most underestimated phase. Combining development teams, governance cultures, technical infrastructure, and community expectations requires sustained effort over 6-18 months. Integration is covered in depth in Chapter 10.

**Key Deliverable:** Integration Playbook, 100-Day Plan, Performance Tracking Dashboard.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Overlapping Phases**

The phases overlap significantly. Target screening begins while strategy is being finalized. Due diligence overlaps with valuation refinement. Negotiation continues during governance preparation. The investment banker must manage multiple workstreams simultaneously.
{% endhint %}

## 7.2.3 Key Differences from Traditional M&A

| Dimension | Traditional M&A | DAO M&A |
|-----------|----------------|---------|
| **Decision authority** | Board of directors | Token holder governance vote |
| **Information access** | Confidential data rooms | Most data publicly available on-chain |
| **Confidentiality** | NDA-protected until announcement | May require public governance discussion pre-deal |
| **Approval process** | Board vote + regulatory approval | Community discussion + governance vote + quorum |
| **Deal certainty** | High once board approves | Uncertain until governance vote closes |
| **Integration authority** | CEO mandate | Governance-approved integration committee |
| **Exit risk** | Limited (shareholders can sell) | Fork risk (community can replicate the protocol) |
| **Timeline variability** | Moderate (regulatory-driven) | High (governance-driven) |

---

## Key Takeaways

- The DAO M&A process follows the same general sequence as traditional M&A but with critical modifications at every stage
- Eight phases span 9-18 months from initiation to integration completion
- Governance approval is the most unpredictable phase and requires dedicated campaign strategy
- On-chain transparency enables public due diligence but complicates confidential deal-making
- The investment banker must manage overlapping workstreams and simultaneous stakeholder engagement
- Fork risk — the ability of disaffected communities to replicate the protocol — has no equivalent in traditional M&A
