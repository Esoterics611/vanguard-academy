# 9.3 Fairness Opinions for DAO Transactions

In traditional M&A, a fairness opinion is a formal opinion from an investment bank that the transaction consideration is fair, from a financial point of view, to the company's shareholders. In DAO M&A, fairness opinions serve the practical function of providing token holders with independent analytical support — significantly increasing the probability of governance approval.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Explain the role and scope of a fairness opinion in DAO M&A
- Construct a fairness opinion using DCF, comparable, and precedent transaction analysis
- Present a fairness conclusion with supporting quantitative analysis
{% endhint %}

---

## 9.5.1 The Role of the Fairness Opinion

In traditional M&A, fairness opinions serve both a **legal function** (evidence of fiduciary duty) and a **practical function** (credibility for shareholder approval).

In DAO M&A, the legal function is less clear — no board of directors exists to whom fiduciary duty is owed in the traditional sense. But the practical function is critical: a credible fairness opinion significantly increases governance approval probability by demonstrating that the exchange ratio has been independently validated.

## 9.5.2 Fairness Opinion Methodology

A fairness opinion for a DAO transaction should include:

| Component | Content |
|-----------|---------|
| **Scope** | What the opinion covers (fairness of exchange ratio) and does not cover (strategic merits, tax, legal) |
| **Valuation Summary** | Independent valuations of both entities using multiple methodologies |
| **Synergy Assessment** | Independent assessment of expected synergies and their present value |
| **Premium Analysis** | Comparison to premiums in precedent transactions |
| **Conclusion** | Formal statement that the exchange ratio is fair from a financial point of view |

## 9.5.3 Case Study: Fairness Opinion for a Hypothetical DEX Merger

Two DEX protocols, **"Alpha"** and **"Beta,"** propose to merge. Alpha is the larger entity acquiring Beta through a token swap.

### Protocol Profiles

| Metric | Alpha (Acquirer) | Beta (Target) |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| FDV | $1.5 billion | $200 million |
| Annual revenue | $40 million | $8 million |
| TVL | $2 billion | $300 million |
| Daily volume | $300 million | $50 million |
| Token supply | 100M ALPHA | 200M BETA |
| Token price | $15 | $1.00 |
| Specialization | Broad market DEX | Concentrated liquidity on niche pairs |

**Proposed Exchange Ratio:** 0.08 ALPHA per BETA

**Implied value:** 0.08 x $15 = **$1.20 per BETA** (20% premium to market price of $1.00)

### Fairness Analysis

#### DCF Valuation of Beta

Using Part II methodology, Beta's standalone DCF value ranges from **$180-280 million** depending on assumptions. At 200 million tokens, this implies **$0.90-$1.40 per BETA**.

#### Comparable Analysis

Beta's P/R of 25x ($200M FDV / $8M revenue) is within the DEX peer group range of 15-50x.

```
At peer group median P/R of 20x:  $8M × 20 = $160M  ($0.80 per BETA)
At premium P/R of 30x:            $8M × 30 = $240M  ($1.20 per BETA)
```

A mid-range multiple is warranted given Beta's niche specialization and moderate growth profile.

#### Precedent Transaction Analysis

Prior DEX-related transactions suggest premiums of 15-35% over standalone values.

```
At 25% premium to DCF midpoint ($230M):      $287M  ($1.44 per BETA)
At 25% premium to comparable midpoint ($200M): $250M  ($1.25 per BETA)
```

#### Synergy Assessment

| Synergy Type | Annual Value | Valuation (25x) |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Revenue synergies (cross-liquidity) | $15 million | $375 million |
| Cost synergies (duplicate infrastructure) | $3 million | $75 million |
| **Total synergies** | **$18 million** | **$450 million** |

Beta's 60% share of synergy value = **$270 million**.

### Exchange Ratio Analysis

At 0.08 ALPHA per BETA:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Implied BETA value | 0.08 x $15 = **$1.20** |
| Total implied value for BETA | $1.20 x 200M = **$240 million** |
| Premium to market | **20%** ($1.20 vs. $1.00) |
| Premium to DCF midpoint | **4%** ($240M vs. $230M) |
| Relative to comp range | Within fair range ($160-240M) |

### Fairness Opinion Conclusion

{% hint style="success" %}
**Formal Conclusion**

Based on the foregoing analysis, the proposed exchange ratio of 0.08 ALPHA per BETA is within the range of fair values implied by DCF, comparable, and precedent transaction analyses. The 20% premium to market price is consistent with premiums observed in strategic transactions. We are of the opinion that, **from a financial point of view, the exchange ratio is fair to the holders of BETA tokens**.
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- Fairness opinions are not legally required for DAOs but practically increase governance approval probability
- The opinion should use multiple methodologies (DCF, comps, precedent transactions) and reconcile the results
- Synergy assessment and allocation are integral to exchange ratio analysis
- Premium analysis against precedent transactions validates reasonableness
- The formal conclusion states that the ratio is fair "from a financial point of view" — not that it is the best possible deal
- A visual valuation range (football field) effectively communicates the fairness analysis
