# 10.2 Technical, Treasury, and Community Integration

This section covers the three dimensions of integration that determine long-term success: technical architecture decisions, treasury consolidation strategy, and the cultural alignment that is the #1 predictor of merger outcomes.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Select and justify a technical integration architecture model
- Design a phased treasury consolidation approach
- Identify cultural dimensions that require assessment and alignment
- Develop an integration communication strategy
- Establish KPIs to measure integration success across four dimensions
{% endhint %}

## 10.3 Technical Integration Strategies

### 10.3.1 Integration Architecture Models

| Model | Description | Risk Level | Best When |
|-------|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Coexistence** | Separate smart contracts, unified governance and treasury | Lowest | Protocols serve different functions |
| **Wrapper** | One protocol wraps the other for integrated UX | Moderate | User-facing integration needed, contracts are battle-tested |
| **Migration** | Target functionality rebuilt in acquirer's framework | Highest | Deep integration required, sufficient engineering time |

### Coexistence Model

Both protocols continue operating as separate smart contract systems, with integration at governance and treasury levels only.

**Example:** A lending protocol acquires an oracle network. The oracle contracts operate independently; integration occurs through preferential pricing and unified governance.

### Wrapper Model

One protocol wraps the other, creating integrated user experience while maintaining separate underlying contracts.

**Example:** A DEX acquires a lending protocol. The DEX frontend adds a "Lend" tab that interfaces with the lending protocol's contracts. Users see a unified experience; contracts remain separate and independently auditable.

### Migration Model

Target functionality rebuilt within the acquirer's smart contract framework, with users migrated from old to new contracts.

**Example:** Aave's V2 to V3 migration — users incentivized to move from old contracts to new ones.

{% hint style="success" %}
**Recommended Approach**

Start with the **Coexistence Model**, progress to the **Wrapper Model** as confidence builds, and consider the **Migration Model** only when the combined engineering team has had sufficient time to design, audit, and test the integrated system.
{% endhint %}

## 10.3.2 Treasury Integration

Treasury integration should follow a phased approach:

**Phase 1 — Multi-sig Consolidation:** Establish a unified multi-sig (or governance-controlled) treasury wallet with signers from both communities. Transfer liquid stablecoin holdings to the unified wallet.

**Phase 2 — DeFi Position Unwind:** Gradually unwind the target's DeFi positions (LP positions, lending positions, yield strategies) and transfer proceeds to the unified treasury. Conduct carefully to avoid market impact and minimize gas costs.

**Phase 3 — Unified Treasury Strategy:** Implement a unified treasury management strategy — asset allocation, risk management, yield optimization — all approved through the new governance process.

## 10.4 Community Integration

### 10.4.1 The Cultural Challenge

The most common cause of DAO M&A failure is cultural incompatibility. Two communities with different values, communication styles, and governance expectations will struggle under unified governance.

**Cultural Dimensions to Assess and Align:**

| Dimension | Question | Conflict Example |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| **Decision-Making Speed** | Deliberate consensus vs. rapid iteration? | One side wants 2-week discussions; the other wants 48-hour votes |
| **Compensation Philosophy** | Generous tokens vs. treasury preservation? | Target contributors expect 2x what acquirer pays |
| **Transparency Expectations** | Total transparency vs. selective confidentiality? | Target community demands all salaries be public |
| **Technical Standards** | Different code quality, testing, and documentation norms? | Teams disagree on whether 80% or 95% test coverage is acceptable |

### 10.4.2 Integration Communication Strategy

Communication is the primary tool for managing community integration:

**Over-communicate:** Provide weekly integration updates through governance forums, community calls, and social media. Silence breeds anxiety and rumors.

**Acknowledge uncertainty:** Don't pretend integration is easy or that all questions have answers. Transparently communicate challenges and how they are being addressed.

**Celebrate early wins:** Identify and publicize early successes — first combined product feature, first cost synergy realized, first joint governance proposal passed.

**Create shared identity:** Develop messaging that frames the combined entity as greater than the sum of its parts. A compelling shared vision helps both communities move beyond pre-merger identities.

**Maintain channels for dissent:** Provide structured mechanisms for voicing concerns. Suppressing dissent drives it underground and creates governance toxicity.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**The Fork Threat**

In traditional M&A, disaffected employees can leave but cannot replicate the company. In DAO M&A, disaffected community members can fork the protocol and compete with the merged entity. Community integration is not just about harmony — it is about preventing existential competitive threats.
{% endhint %}

## 10.5 Measuring Integration Success

### 10.5.1 Integration KPIs

Track metrics across four dimensions:

#### Financial KPIs
- Combined protocol revenue vs. sum of standalone revenues
- Cost run-rate vs. pre-merger combined costs
- Synergy realization rate (actual vs. projected)
- Treasury balance and composition
- Token price performance vs. crypto market benchmark

#### Operational KPIs
- TVL retention (combined vs. sum of pre-merger TVL)
- User retention (active users vs. pre-merger combined)
- Development velocity (features shipped, bugs fixed)
- Security incidents post-merger

#### Governance KPIs
- Governance participation rate (is the combined community engaging?)
- Proposal success rate (are proposals passing?)
- Voter turnout from both pre-merger communities (is one side disengaging?)
- Governance conflict frequency

#### Community KPIs
- Discord/Telegram active member count and engagement
- Social media sentiment analysis
- Contributor retention rate (especially key persons)
- New contributor recruitment rate

{% hint style="info" %}
**Leading vs. Lagging Indicators**

Community and governance KPIs are **leading indicators** — they predict future financial outcomes. If governance participation is declining and community sentiment is negative, financial deterioration will follow. Monitor leading indicators closely during the first 100 days.
{% endhint %}

## Key Takeaways

- Three technical integration models offer increasing depth and risk — start with coexistence, progress carefully
- Treasury integration follows three phases: multi-sig consolidation, DeFi position unwind, unified strategy
- Cultural incompatibility is the #1 cause of DAO M&A failure — assess and plan for it explicitly
- Over-communicate during integration; silence breeds anxiety and rumors
- The fork threat makes community integration existentially important — not just nice to have
- Track KPIs across four dimensions; community and governance metrics are leading indicators of financial outcomes
