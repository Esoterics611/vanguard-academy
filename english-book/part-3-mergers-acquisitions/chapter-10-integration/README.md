# Chapter 10: Post-Merger Integration

Research from McKinsey, BCG, and Bain consistently finds that 60-70% of mergers fail to achieve their expected synergies, and a significant percentage destroy value. The primary cause is not poor strategic logic or excessive price — it is poor post-merger integration execution.

In the DAO context, integration is even more challenging: no CEO to mandate decisions, pseudonymous decentralized contributors, smart contract integration risk, community persuasion at scale, and the ever-present fork threat.

## Chapter Overview

This chapter provides the integration playbook for DAO M&A:

- **The 100-day plan** — structured phases from preparation through optimization
- **Technical integration** — three architecture models with increasing depth and risk
- **Treasury and governance integration** — phased approaches to financial and organizational unification
- **Community and cultural integration** — the most common cause of DAO M&A failure
- **Measuring success** — KPIs across financial, operational, governance, and community dimensions
- **The Fei/Rari post-mortem** — five critical lessons for integration planning

## Key Concepts

{% hint style="danger" %}
**The #1 Cause of Failure**

The most common cause of DAO M&A failure is not strategic logic or pricing — it is cultural integration. Two communities with different values, communication styles, and governance expectations will struggle to coexist under unified governance. Assess cultural compatibility before committing.
{% endhint %}

By the end of this chapter, you should be able to:

- [ ] Design a 100-day integration plan with clear phase objectives and deliverables
- [ ] Select an appropriate technical integration architecture (coexistence, wrapper, migration)
- [ ] Plan a phased treasury consolidation approach
- [ ] Identify and mitigate cultural integration risks before they derail the merger
- [ ] Establish integration KPIs across four dimensions (financial, operational, governance, community)
- [ ] Apply the five lessons from the Fei/Rari failure to future transactions
