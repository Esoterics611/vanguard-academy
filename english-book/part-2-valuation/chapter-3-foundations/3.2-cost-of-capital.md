# 3.2 The Cost of Capital in Decentralized Finance

Every DCF analysis requires a discount rate that reflects the riskiness of the cash flows being valued. In traditional corporate finance, the cost of capital is estimated using the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM) and the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC). Adapting these frameworks to the DAO context requires careful consideration of the unique risk factors affecting decentralized protocols.

## 3.2.1 The Risk-Free Rate in Crypto

The risk-free rate in traditional CAPM represents the return on a theoretically riskless investment—typically U.S. Treasury securities. In the crypto context, there is no truly "risk-free" rate, but several candidates exist:

### Option 1: U.S. Treasury Yields

**Description:** The traditional risk-free rate remains relevant for crypto valuations denominated in USD. If we are estimating the present value of USD-denominated cash flows from a protocol, the U.S. Treasury yield curve is the appropriate starting point.

**Current Rate:** As of late 2024, the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield is approximately 4.0-4.5%.

**When to Use:** USD-denominated protocol valuations

### Option 2: ETH Staking Yield

**Description:** For valuations denominated in ETH (relevant when protocol revenue is earned in ETH and valued in ETH terms), the ETH staking yield represents something analogous to the risk-free rate—it is the baseline return available to ETH holders without taking protocol-specific risk.

**Current Rate:** As of 2024, the ETH staking yield is approximately 3.0-4.0% annualized.

**When to Use:** ETH-denominated protocol valuations

### Option 3: DeFi Lending Rates on Stablecoins

**Description:** The yield on lending USDC or DAI through major, battle-tested lending protocols (Aave, Compound) represents a low-risk (but not zero-risk) return on capital in the DeFi ecosystem.

**Typical Range:** 2-8% depending on market conditions, with a long-run average around 3-5%.

**When to Use:** DeFi-native valuations requiring on-chain reference rates

{% hint style="success" %}
**Recommendation**

For most DAO valuation exercises, we recommend using the **U.S. Treasury yield** as the risk-free rate when valuing in USD terms, and noting the ETH staking yield as a reference point for ETH-denominated analysis.
{% endhint %}

## 3.2.2 The Equity Risk Premium for Crypto Protocols

The equity risk premium (ERP) represents the additional return investors require for bearing the risk of investing in equities rather than risk-free assets. For the U.S. equity market, the historical ERP is approximately **5-7%** (depending on the measurement period and methodology, as extensively debated by Damodaran, Dimson-Marsh-Staunton, and others).

For crypto protocols, the risk premium must be significantly higher to reflect:

### Smart Contract Risk

**Description:** The risk that a bug, exploit, or vulnerability in the protocol's smart contracts results in partial or total loss of value.

**Evidence:** The history of DeFi exploits is extensive—over $5 billion has been lost to hacks and exploits since DeFi's inception. Major incidents include:
- The DAO hack (2016, $60 million)
- Wormhole bridge exploit (2022, $320 million)
- Euler Finance exploit (2023, $197 million)
- Mango Markets manipulation (2022, $116 million)

**Premium:** Even well-audited protocols carry residual smart contract risk.

### Regulatory Risk

**Description:** The risk that regulatory action restricts or prohibits protocol operations.

**Evidence:** SEC enforcement actions against Ripple, LBRY, and various DeFi protocols demonstrate that regulatory risk is not theoretical. The classification of tokens as securities, restrictions on DeFi activities, or mandatory KYC requirements could fundamentally impair protocol value.

### Governance Risk

**Description:** The risk that governance dysfunction—voter apathy, plutocratic control, governance attacks, or strategic mismanagement—destroys protocol value.

**Evidence:** The Beanstalk governance attack (2022, $182 million stolen through a flash loan governance exploit) illustrates the extreme tail of this risk.

### Market and Liquidity Risk

**Description:** Crypto markets are significantly more volatile than traditional equity markets.

**Evidence:** Bitcoin's annualized volatility has historically ranged from 50-100%, and altcoin volatility is typically higher. Token liquidity can evaporate rapidly during market stress.

### Technology Risk

**Description:** The risk that the underlying blockchain infrastructure (Layer 1, Layer 2, bridges) experiences outages, exploits, or obsolescence that impairs the protocol.

### Key Person Risk

**Description:** Many protocols remain heavily dependent on founding teams and core contributors. The departure of key developers can significantly impair protocol development and value.

## Estimation Approaches for the Crypto Risk Premium

### Approach 1: Build-up Method

Start with the traditional ERP (5-7%) and add premiums for each category of additional risk.

**Example Build-up for a Mature, Well-Audited DeFi Protocol:**

| Risk Component | Premium |
|----------------|---------|
| Traditional ERP | 6% |
| Smart Contract Risk | 3-5% |
| Regulatory Risk | 3-5% |
| Governance Risk | 2-3% |
| Market/Liquidity Risk | 3-5% |
| Technology Risk | 1-2% |
| Key Person Risk | 1-3% |
| **Total Risk Premium** | **19-29%** |

**Implied Total Cost of Equity:** 23-34% (adding the ~4% risk-free rate)

### Approach 2: Implied from Market Multiples

We can reverse-engineer the market's implied discount rate from observable price-to-earnings or price-to-revenue multiples.

**Example:** If a protocol trades at 10x annual revenue and revenue is expected to grow at 30% annually, the implied discount rate can be extracted from a simple growth model.

**Advantage:** Market-derived, reflects current investor sentiment
**Disadvantage:** May reflect irrational exuberance or depression

### Approach 3: Volatility-Based Approach

Using the protocol token's realized volatility relative to a market benchmark to estimate beta, then applying a modified CAPM.

**Example:**
- If ETH has an annualized volatility of 60% and a beta of roughly 1.5 relative to the S&P 500
- And a specific DeFi token has a beta of 1.8 relative to ETH
- The token's total systematic risk can be estimated and translated into a required return

{% hint style="info" %}
**Recommended Framework**

For the valuation exercises in this handbook, we recommend using a **base cost of equity between 25% and 40%** for DeFi protocols, with the specific rate selected based on:
- Protocol maturity
- Audit history
- Regulatory exposure
- Governance quality

This range is deliberately wide—the analyst must exercise judgment within it based on protocol-specific risk assessment.
{% endhint %}

### Sensitivity Analysis Requirement

Given the uncertainty in cost of capital estimation, **every DCF valuation in this handbook should present results across a range of discount rates** (typically ±5-10 percentage points around the base case) to demonstrate value sensitivity to this assumption.

## 3.2.3 The Terminal Value Problem

In traditional DCF analysis, the terminal value—the value of all cash flows beyond the explicit forecast period—typically represents **60-80% of total estimated value**. Terminal value is usually estimated using either a perpetuity growth model (Gordon Growth Model) or an exit multiple approach.

For crypto protocols, the terminal value problem is particularly acute:

### Challenge 1: Perpetuity Assumptions

**Issue:** Perpetuity assumptions are challenging when the protocol's competitive position may be fundamentally disrupted by technology evolution.

**Implication:** Assuming a DeFi protocol will generate cash flows in perpetuity requires a belief that the protocol will maintain relevance through multiple technology cycles—a stronger assumption than the equivalent for a traditional company.

### Challenge 2: Exit Multiple Selection

**Issue:** Exit multiple selection is circular without a robust set of comparable company multiples.

**Implication:** The crypto market is too young and volatile to provide reliable long-run multiple benchmarks.

## Recommended Terminal Value Approach

{% hint style="success" %}
**Best Practice Framework**

1. **Use shorter explicit forecast periods:** 5-7 years rather than 10

2. **Terminal value via scenario analysis:**
   - **Base case:** Protocol maintains competitive position
   - **Bull case:** Protocol captures additional market share
   - **Bear case:** Protocol faces competitive displacement or regulatory impairment

3. **Weight scenarios by probability** to derive an expected terminal value

4. **Cap terminal value at no more than 60%** of total DCF value for crypto protocols
   - If terminal value exceeds this threshold, the valuation is excessively dependent on long-term assumptions that carry enormous uncertainty
   - Extend the explicit forecast period or revise growth assumptions to reduce terminal value dependence
{% endhint %}

### Terminal Value Formula

Using the Gordon Growth Model:

```
Terminal Value = (Final Year PFCF × (1 + g)) / (r - g)

Where:
g = Terminal growth rate (typically 2-3%, reflecting long-run GDP growth)
r = Discount rate (cost of equity)
```

**Example:**
```
Year 5 PFCF: $619M
Terminal growth rate (g): 3%
Discount rate (r): 30%

Terminal Value = ($619M × 1.03) / (0.30 - 0.03)
               = $637.6M / 0.27
               = $2,361M
```

## Key Takeaways

✅ Risk-free rate options: U.S. Treasury yields (4-4.5%), ETH staking (3-4%), DeFi lending (3-5%)
✅ Crypto protocols require significantly higher risk premiums than traditional equities
✅ Recommended cost of equity range: **25-40%** based on protocol-specific risk factors
✅ Three estimation approaches: build-up method, market-implied, volatility-based
✅ Terminal value requires scenario analysis and should not exceed 60% of total value
✅ Always perform sensitivity analysis across discount rate assumptions
