# 5.1 The Comparable Company Analysis Framework

Comparable company analysis (comps) values a subject asset by reference to the valuation multiples at which similar assets trade. In traditional equity analysis, comps are constructed by identifying a peer group of similar companies, calculating relevant valuation multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Revenue), and applying the peer group's median or mean multiples to the subject company's financial metrics.

For protocols, the framework is identical in structure but requires adaptation in peer selection criteria and multiple selection.

## 5.1.1 Peer Group Selection

Defining an appropriate peer group requires matching on multiple dimensions:

### Economic Function

**The most important dimension.** A DEX should be compared to other DEXs, not to lending protocols. A lending protocol should be compared to other lending protocols, not to oracle networks.

**Category mapping:**
- **DEX peers:** Uniswap, Curve, SushiSwap, PancakeSwap
- **Lending peers:** Aave, Compound, Euler, Morpho
- **Liquid staking peers:** Lido, Rocket Pool, Frax Ether

{% hint style="info" %}
**Why Economic Function Matters**

Protocols in different categories have fundamentally different business models, risk profiles, and value accrual mechanisms. A DEX generates revenue from trading volume, while a lending protocol generates revenue from interest spreads. These cannot be meaningfully compared using the same multiples.
{% endhint %}

### Market Position and Size

A large, dominant protocol should be compared to other large protocols, not to early-stage competitors. Market cap and TVL are useful size screens.

**Size tiers:**
- **Tier 1:** Market cap > $5B, TVL > $5B (e.g., Uniswap, Lido, Aave)
- **Tier 2:** Market cap $500M-$5B, TVL $1B-$5B
- **Tier 3:** Market cap < $500M, TVL < $1B

### Growth Profile

High-growth protocols should be compared to other high-growth protocols. Mature, slow-growth protocols should be compared to peers at similar stages.

**Growth classification:**
- **High growth:** >50% YoY revenue growth
- **Medium growth:** 20-50% YoY revenue growth
- **Mature:** <20% YoY revenue growth

### Chain and Ecosystem

Multi-chain protocols may warrant different multiples than single-chain protocols. Ethereum-native protocols may trade at premiums to protocols on alternative Layer 1s due to:
- Security benefits
- Liquidity depth
- Ecosystem integration advantages

### Revenue Quality

Protocols with sustainable, non-incentivized revenue should be distinguished from those whose activity is primarily driven by token incentives.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Organic Revenue Assessment**

One useful metric is **"organic revenue"**—revenue that would persist if token incentive programs were eliminated. Many protocols report impressive revenue figures that collapse when incentives end.
{% endhint %}

## 5.1.2 Valuation Multiples for DeFi Protocols

### Price-to-Revenue (P/R or Market Cap / Annualized Revenue)

The most widely used DeFi valuation multiple due to the difficulty of estimating earnings for protocols where expense accounting is non-standardized.

**Calculation:**
```
P/R = Fully Diluted Valuation / Annualized Protocol Revenue
```

**As of 2024, typical P/R ranges:**

| Category | P/R Range | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|-----------|
| Established DEXs (Uniswap, Curve) | 10-30x | Mature markets, competitive pressure |
| Lending protocols (Aave, Compound) | 10-25x | Stable revenue, moderate growth |
| Liquid staking (Lido) | 15-40x | High growth, network effects |
| Infrastructure/Oracles (Chainlink) | 50-100x+ | Growth expectations, network premium |
| Stablecoin issuers (MakerDAO/Sky) | 5-15x | Bank-like revenue, regulatory risk |

{% hint style="success" %}
**Why P/R Is the Primary Multiple**

Unlike traditional companies with standardized accounting, DeFi protocols lack consistent expense reporting. Many protocols have minimal operating expenses (smart contracts run autonomously), making revenue a more reliable basis for comparison than earnings.
{% endhint %}

### Price-to-Earnings (P/E or Market Cap / Net Protocol Income)

Where net income can be estimated (revenue minus all operating expenses including token-based compensation at fair value), P/E provides a more refined multiple.

**Limitation:** The lack of standardized expense reporting makes P/E less reliable than P/R for cross-protocol comparison.

**Typical P/E ranges for profitable protocols:** 15-50x (reflecting high growth expectations and premium for scarcity of profitable DeFi protocols)

### Enterprise Value to Revenue (EV/R)

Enterprise Value for a protocol is calculated as:

```
EV = Market Cap + Debt - Cash and Liquid Treasury Assets (excluding native tokens)
```

This adjusts for treasury holdings and is more appropriate when comparing protocols with very different treasury sizes.

{% hint style="info" %}
**When to Use EV/R**

Use EV/R when:
- Comparing protocols with materially different treasury sizes
- The protocol has significant debt obligations
- Treasury assets are large relative to market cap
{% endhint %}

### Total Value Locked (TVL) Multiples

**Market Cap / TVL** provides a measure of how efficiently the protocol monetizes its locked capital.

**Interpretation:**
- **Lower ratios:** Suggest either undervaluation or low capital efficiency
- **Higher ratios:** Suggest premium valuation or superior monetization

{% hint style="warning" %}
**TVL Multiple Limitations**

This metric is most useful within homogeneous categories (comparing lending protocol to lending protocol). Do not compare TVL multiples across different protocol types—a DEX and a lending protocol use TVL completely differently.
{% endhint %}

### Revenue/TVL (Capital Efficiency Ratio)

This is not a valuation multiple per se but a **quality metric** that helps justify premium or discount multiples.

**Principle:** Protocols that generate more revenue per unit of TVL are more capital-efficient and deserve higher valuation multiples.

**Example:**
- Protocol A: $100M revenue, $2B TVL → 5% Revenue/TVL
- Protocol B: $100M revenue, $5B TVL → 2% Revenue/TVL

Protocol A is more capital-efficient and should trade at a premium to Protocol B.

### Fee Generation Rate

**For DEXs:** The ratio of trading fees to trading volume indicates pricing power.

**For lending protocols:** The ratio of protocol revenue to outstanding borrows indicates margin.

These efficiency metrics help explain multiple differentials within peer groups.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Peer group selection is critical** - Match on economic function first, then size, growth, chain, and revenue quality
2. **P/R is the primary multiple** for DeFi protocols due to lack of standardized expense accounting
3. **TVL multiples must be used carefully** - Only compare within homogeneous protocol categories
4. **Capital efficiency metrics** (Revenue/TVL) justify premium or discount valuations
5. **Multiple ranges vary significantly** by protocol category - Infrastructure commands highest multiples, stablecoins lowest
