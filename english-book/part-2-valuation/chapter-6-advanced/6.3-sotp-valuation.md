# 6.3 Sum-of-the-Parts Valuation

Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP) valuation is appropriate when a protocol operates **multiple distinct business lines** that have different risk/return profiles and could theoretically be valued independently.

## 6.3.1 When SOTP Is Appropriate

### Ideal Candidates for SOTP

**Multi-product protocols** where:
1. Business lines serve different markets or customers
2. Revenue streams have different growth rates and risk profiles
3. Each component could theoretically operate independently
4. Components use different economic models

### Examples of SOTP Candidates

**MakerDAO/Sky with SubDAO structure:**
- Each SubDAO (Spark, farming strategies, RWA allocation) operates semi-independently
- Different risk profiles: crypto collateral vs. RWA vs. stablecoin operations
- Can be valued using methodology appropriate to each business model

**Multi-product protocols like Aave:**
- Core lending markets (established, stable growth)
- GHO stablecoin (early stage, high growth potential)
- Governance and protocol services
- Each generates different revenue with different characteristics

**Layer 2 ecosystems like Optimism:**
- Sequencer revenue (infrastructure business)
- Governance token value (ecosystem coordination)
- Ecosystem grants and development (strategic value)

{% hint style="info" %}
**SOTP vs. Consolidated Valuation**

**Use consolidated DCF when:**
- Single business model
- Integrated operations
- Unified revenue stream

**Use SOTP when:**
- Multiple distinct businesses
- Different growth/risk profiles
- Components could be separated or sold independently
{% endhint %}

## 6.3.2 SOTP Methodology

### Step 1: Segment the Business

Identify distinct business components:
- What economic function does each serve?
- What are the revenue drivers?
- Could this component operate independently?

### Step 2: Value Each Component

Apply the **most appropriate valuation method** for each component:
- **Mature, cash-generating:** DCF or revenue multiples
- **Growth stage:** Comparable company multiples
- **RWA/treasury:** Mark-to-market or discounted cash flow
- **Strategic/ecosystem:** Real options or market benchmarks

### Step 3: Sum Component Values

```
Protocol Value = Σ(Component Values) + Treasury + Adjustments
```

### Step 4: Apply Adjustments

Consider:
- **Conglomerate discount:** -10-20% if components lack synergies
- **Integration premium:** +10-20% if components have strong synergies
- **Corporate costs:** Subtract shared overhead not allocated to components

## 6.3.3 Case Study: MakerDAO/Sky Sum-of-the-Parts

MakerDAO (rebranded as Sky) operates multiple distinct business lines, making it an ideal SOTP candidate.

### Component 1: Crypto-Collateralized Lending

**Business description:**
- Traditional DeFi lending: users deposit crypto (ETH, stETH, etc.) as collateral
- Borrow DAI/USDS stablecoin
- Earn stability fees (interest)

**Financial metrics:**
- Estimated annual revenue: **$50 million**
- Growth rate: Low (mature market, declining share)
- Risk profile: Medium (smart contract risk, collateral volatility)

**Valuation approach:**
- **Multiple:** 12-15x revenue (mature, lower-growth business line)
- **Rationale:** Comparable to mature lending protocols
- **Value range:** **$600-750 million**

### Component 2: Real-World Asset (RWA) Revenue

**Business description:**
- Collateral backed by traditional financial assets
- US Treasury bills, corporate bonds, other RWAs
- Generates yield from traditional finance

**Financial metrics:**
- Estimated annual revenue: **$150 million**
- Growth rate: Medium (expanding RWA allocation)
- Risk profile: Lower (backed by traditional assets, but regulatory risk)

**Valuation approach:**
- **Multiple:** 8-12x revenue
- **Rationale:** Lower than pure DeFi (more predictable, but faces TradFi competition and regulatory risk)
- **Value range:** **$1,200-1,800 million**

{% hint style="success" %}
**Why Lower Multiple for RWA?**

RWA business lines trade at lower multiples than pure DeFi because:
- More predictable (less upside)
- Compete with traditional finance (lower margins)
- Regulatory risk (more scrutiny)
- Less "pure crypto" premium

But they're more stable and institutional-friendly.
{% endhint %}

### Component 3: Spark Protocol (Lending Market)

**Business description:**
- Fork of Aave, operated as SubDAO
- Full-featured lending market
- Integrated with MakerDAO ecosystem

**Financial metrics:**
- Estimated annual revenue: **$30 million**
- Growth rate: High (growth-stage protocol)
- Risk profile: Medium (newer protocol, but proven technology)

**Valuation approach:**
- **Multiple:** 15-20x revenue
- **Rationale:** Growth-stage lending protocol with MakerDAO support
- **Value range:** **$450-600 million**

### Component 4: DSR/SSR Revenue (Savings Rate)

**Business description:**
- DAI/USDS Savings Rate: interest paid to stablecoin holders
- Revenue = spread between yield earned on reserves and rate paid out
- May be positive or negative depending on competitive environment

**Financial metrics:**
- Estimated net contribution: **$20-50 million**
- Highly variable based on market conditions

**Valuation approach:**
- **Multiple:** 10x (moderate quality, cyclical)
- **Value range:** **$200-500 million**

{% hint style="warning" %}
**DSR Can Be Negative**

When competing for stablecoin market share, MakerDAO may pay more in DSR than it earns on deployed capital. This component could be a **cost center** rather than revenue generator.

In competitive environments (high DeFi yields), DSR is negative. In calm markets (low competing yields), DSR is positive.
{% endhint %}

### Component 5: Treasury Value

**Holdings:**
- Stablecoins (USDC, USDT)
- ETH
- Protocol-owned liquidity
- Other assets

**Valuation:**
- **Mark-to-market:** Current market value
- Exclude MKR/SKY tokens (not liquid, circular)
- **Estimated value:** **$200-400 million**

### Component 6: Brand and Ecosystem Value

**Intangible assets:**
- MakerDAO/Sky brand value
- Regulatory positioning (one of oldest, most established)
- Ecosystem partnerships
- Developer mindshare

**Valuation approach:**
- Difficult to quantify directly
- **Captured implicitly** through premium multiples on revenue-generating components
- Or added as separate premium: $100-300M

**SOTP Reveals Diversification Value**

- Not dependent on single revenue stream

The market should value this diversification positively (lower risk) or negatively (conglomerate discount), depending on execution.
{% endhint %}

## 6.3.5 SOTP Adjustments

### Synergy Premium

If components work better together than separately, add **synergy premium:**

**Positive synergies:**
- Shared liquidity (DAI used across all components)
- Brand halo effect
- Integrated user experience
- Technical infrastructure sharing

**Estimated synergy value:** +10-15% premium

### Conglomerate Discount

If components are unrelated or poorly integrated, apply **conglomerate discount:**

**Reasons for discount:**
- Management complexity
- Diluted strategic focus
- Investors prefer pure-play exposure
- Integration overhead

**Typical discount:** -10-20%

### MakerDAO Assessment

**Verdict:** Slight premium justified (+5-10%)

**Rationale:**
- Components share DAI stablecoin (strong integration)
- SubDAO structure allows specialized management
- Diversification reduces risk
- Brand value applies across components

## 6.3.6 Comparing SOTP to Market Price

### Market Price Analysis (Example)

Assume MKR trades at **$2,800** with 900,000 tokens:
```
Market cap = $2,800 × 900,000 = $2,520 million
```

### SOTP vs. Market

| Method | Value | Per Token |
|--------|-------|-----------|
| SOTP Low | $2,650M | $2,944 |
| SOTP Mid | $3,350M | $3,722 |
| SOTP High | $4,050M | $4,500 |
| **Market Price** | **$2,520M** | **$2,800** |

**Interpretation:** Market is trading **below SOTP low estimate**

**Possible explanations:**
1. Market applies conglomerate discount we didn't
2. Market is more pessimistic on revenue estimates
3. Market sees execution risk in SubDAO structure
4. Market assigns lower multiples across the board
5. **Potential undervaluation opportunity**

{% hint style="success" %}
**Investment Implications**

If you have confidence in:
- Revenue estimates
- Multiple selection
- Component valuations

Then SOTP suggests MKR is **undervalued at $2,800**.

Fair value: **$3,300-3,700** (midpoint range)
**Upside:** 18-32%
{% endhint %}

## 6.3.7 SOTP Best Practices

### Do's

✅ **Use appropriate methodology for each component**
- Don't force all components into same valuation approach
- Match method to component characteristics

✅ **Be granular in segmentation**
- Break down to meaningful business units
- But don't over-segment (diminishing returns)

✅ **Consider synergies and discounts**
- Integration value matters
- Don't just mechanically sum components

### Don'ts

❌ **Don't use SOTP for integrated businesses**
- If operations can't be separated, use consolidated approach

❌ **Don't double-count value**
- Treasury shouldn't be included in component valuations AND added separately
- Shared infrastructure costs should be allocated, not ignored

❌ **Don't ignore execution risk**
- Managing multiple businesses is harder
- May justify discount even if components are valuable

## Key Takeaways

1. **SOTP is for multi-business protocols** - Different revenue streams, risk profiles, and growth rates
2. **Value each component independently** - Use methodology appropriate to that component
3. **MakerDAO is ideal SOTP candidate** - Crypto lending, RWA, Spark, DSR are distinct businesses
4. **Sum components and adjust** - Add synergy premium or conglomerate discount
5. **Compare to market price** - SOTP can reveal under/overvaluation
6. **Integration matters** - Synergies justify premium; poor integration justifies discount
