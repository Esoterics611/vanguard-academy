# 14.1 Market Making and DEX Liquidity

A market maker provides liquidity by continuously posting buy and sell orders, ensuring traders can execute without excessive slippage. For a new token launch, the market maker's role is critical: facilitating price discovery, providing order book depth and stability, and supporting orderly aftermarket trading.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Explain the market maker's role in token launches (price discovery, depth, aftermarket)
- Evaluate market maker proposals using selection criteria
- Negotiate token loan agreements and identify red flags
- Design a protocol-owned DEX liquidity strategy
{% endhint %}

## 14.1.1 The Role of the Market Maker

| Function | Description | IPO Equivalent |
|----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Price Discovery** | Facilitates the market's determination of fair value | Opening price stabilization |
| **Depth and Stability** | Significant orders near current price, reducing trade impact | Underwriter book support |
| **Aftermarket Support** | Ongoing liquidity maintenance post-launch | Stabilization period |

## 14.1.2 Market Maker Selection

Major crypto market makers include Wintermute, GSR, DWF Labs, Jump Crypto, Cumberland, and Keyrock.

**Selection criteria:**

| Criterion | What to Evaluate |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Track record** | How many launches supported? Outcomes (initial performance, sustained liquidity)? |
| **Capital commitment** | How much capital deployed? Larger = deeper liquidity |
| **Market coverage** | Which exchanges (CEX + DEX)? Ideally both |
| **Reputation and ethics** | History of front-running, price manipulation, or wash trading? |

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Vet Carefully**

The crypto market making industry has been plagued by conflicts of interest. Avoid market makers with histories of front-running client orders, manipulating prices, or wash trading. The collapse of Alameda Research serves as the ultimate cautionary tale.
{% endhint %}

## 14.1.3 Market Making Agreement Structures

### Token Loan Model

The protocol lends tokens (typically 2-5% of total supply) for 12-24 months. The market maker uses these plus their own capital for liquidity. At term end, tokens are returned (minus agreed compensation).

**Key negotiation terms:**

| Term | Standard | Watch For |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Loan size | 2-5% of supply | Excessive sizes (more than needed for liquidity) |
| Duration | 12-24 months with renewal option | No return mechanism |
| Performance metrics | 1-2% spread within $50K depth; uptime requirements | No minimum performance requirements |
| Call option | Strike at or above TGE price | Strike significantly below expected market (hidden compensation) |
| Reporting | Regular performance, inventory, and trading reports | No transparency obligations |

{% hint style="danger" %}
**Red Flags in Market Maker Agreements**

- **Call option strike significantly below expected market value** — the market maker extracts value through the option rather than genuine market making
- **No minimum performance requirements** — tokens received with no obligation to provide liquidity
- **Excessive loan sizes** — more tokens than necessary for liquidity provision
- **No clawback or return mechanism** — effectively a token grant disguised as a loan
{% endhint %}

### Retainer Model

The protocol pays a monthly retainer ($50,000-$200,000) plus performance bonuses. No tokens are lent — market maker uses own capital.

**Cleaner** (no lending complications) but **more expensive** in cash and may not be feasible for protocols with limited stablecoin treasury.

## 14.1.4 DEX Liquidity Strategy

### Protocol-Owned Liquidity (POL)

The protocol deploys its own tokens paired with stablecoins or ETH into DEX pools. This creates permanent, protocol-controlled liquidity independent of third-party market makers or mining incentives.

**Sizing:** Sufficient for $500K-$2M daily volume with <2% slippage. On Uniswap V3 or similar concentrated liquidity DEXs, typically requires **$5-20 million** in paired liquidity.

**Pool configuration:**
- **Concentrated ranges** — better capital efficiency but require active management
- **Full-range positions** — require more capital but need less monitoring

### Initial DEX Offering (IDO)

Some protocols launch on a DEX before CEX listings, using the DEX as the primary venue for initial price discovery. Avoids exchange fees and gatekeeping; aligns with decentralized ethos. However, provides less orderly price discovery and may limit initial volume.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Market makers serve three functions: price discovery, depth/stability, and aftermarket support
- Token loan model is standard but must be carefully negotiated — especially the call option
- Red flags: below-market call strikes, no performance minimums, excessive loan sizes, no clawback
- Retainer model is cleaner but more expensive in cash
- Protocol-owned DEX liquidity ($5-20M paired) provides permanent, independent liquidity
- Concentrated liquidity requires active management; full-range is simpler but less capital efficient
