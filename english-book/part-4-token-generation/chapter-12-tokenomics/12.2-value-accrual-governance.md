# 12.2 Value Accrual and Governance Design

Value accrual mechanism design was introduced conceptually in Part II (Chapter 3). Here we provide the practical design framework for implementing each mechanism, plus the governance token parameters that define how the protocol is governed.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Design fee distribution, buyback-burn, ve-model, and staking mechanisms with specific parameters
- Identify the "liquid wrapper problem" and design responses
- Set governance parameters (voting, delegation, quorum, timelock) with justification
- Design emergency governance mechanisms for crisis response
{% endhint %}

## 12.3.1 Designing a Fee Distribution Mechanism

**When to use:** Protocol generates sufficient, sustainable revenue to provide meaningful yield without depleting growth reserves.

**Design parameters:**

| Parameter | Recommendation | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------|-----------|
| Distribution % | 20-40% of revenue initially | Retain 60-80% for growth; increase distribution as protocol matures |
| Eligible recipients | Stakers or governance participants | Incentivizes active engagement; reduces free-rider problem |
| Frequency | Weekly or daily (automated) | Smooth yield; smart contract automation favors shorter intervals |
| Denomination | Stablecoins or ETH | Cleaner for tax/accounting than native token distributions |

**Implementation example:** Aave's Safety Module — AAVE holders stake in the Safety Module (providing economic security), receiving rewards funded by protocol revenue. This simultaneously provides yield, creates a security backstop, reduces circulating supply, and incentivizes long-term holding.

## 12.3.2 Designing a Buyback-and-Burn Mechanism

**When to use:** Return value to all token holders (not just stakers) through supply reduction.

**Design parameters:**

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| Burn trigger | Automatic when revenue exceeds threshold |
| Burn source | Revenue in ETH/stablecoins used to buy tokens on open market |
| Market impact | TWAP execution over hours/days to minimize slippage |
| Transparency | All buybacks publicly trackable on-chain |

**Implementation example:** MakerDAO's Surplus Buffer and Flap Auction — DAI surplus above the buffer target buys and burns MKR through on-chain auctions, creating a direct link between profitability and MKR value.

## 12.3.3 Designing a Vote-Escrow (ve) Mechanism

**When to use:** Incentivize long-term holding, create governance commitment, and build competitive moats.

**Design parameters:**

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| Lock-up range | 1 week to 4 years maximum |
| Decay function | Linear — veToken balance decreases as lock approaches expiration |
| Boost mechanics | Up to 2.5x yield/governance boost (Curve benchmark) |
| Transfer restrictions | Non-transferable (to prevent circumventing lock-up) |

{% hint style="warning" %}
**The Liquid Wrapper Problem**

Convex Finance demonstrated that third-party protocols will create liquid wrappers around locked positions, enabling holders to gain ve benefits without personally committing to a lock. This undermines the governance commitment the ve model is designed to create.

**Design responses:** Require periodic on-chain proof of governance participation to maintain ve benefits, or implement designs that are technically difficult to wrap. However, no perfectly wrapper-resistant design has been achieved — advise clients that liquid wrappers will likely emerge.
{% endhint %}

## 12.3.4 Designing Staking and Security Mechanisms

**When to use:** Protocol requires economic security — financial guarantees that service providers perform correctly or face penalties (slashing).

**Design parameters:**

| Parameter | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| Staking yield | Must attract adequate stake for security (compare to market required yield) |
| Slashing conditions | Clearly defined, objectively verifiable, proportional |
| Slashing magnitude | Sufficient deterrent without discouraging participation |
| Withdrawal delay | 7-21 days (prevents exit before slashing applied) |

**Implementation example:** EigenLayer's restaking model — ETH stakers opt into additional slashing conditions for additional rewards, creating a layered security market where one unit of staked capital secures multiple protocols simultaneously.

## 12.4 Governance Token Design

### 12.4.1 Core Governance Parameters

**Voting Power:**

| Model | Description | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| One-token-one-vote | Simplest; pure plutocracy | Most protocols (well-understood, supported by existing tooling) |
| Quadratic voting | Power = sqrt(tokens); reduces whale dominance | Protocols prioritizing broad participation |
| Conviction voting | Power increases with holding duration | Protocols rewarding persistent preferences |
| Time-weighted | Longer-held tokens get more power | Discouraging short-term governance speculation |

**Recommendation:** One-token-one-vote with delegation for most protocols. Well-understood, supported by Compound Governor / OpenZeppelin Governor tooling.

**Delegation:** Essential at scale. Small holders can assign voting power to representatives without transferring ownership. Consider split delegation (portions to multiple delegates) and topic-specific delegation for sophistication.

**Quorum Requirements:**

| Proposal Type | Recommended Quorum |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Standard proposals | 2-10% of total supply |
| Critical proposals (upgrades, large treasury, security parameters) | 10-20% of total supply |

**Proposal Threshold:** 0.1-1% of total supply required to submit proposals. Delegation should count toward threshold.

**Timelock:**

| Action Type | Recommended Delay |
|------------|------------------|
| Routine parameter changes | 24-48 hours |
| Major upgrades or large treasury disbursements | 7 days |

### 12.4.2 Emergency Governance Mechanisms

**Guardian Multi-sig:** 5-9 trusted individuals with strictly limited emergency powers:
- Can pause contracts (defensive only — not upgrade)
- Subject to retroactive governance ratification within 72 hours
- Powers limited to defensive actions

**Emergency Fast-Track:** Reduced quorum and timelock for proposals tagged as emergencies:
- Clear criteria for what constitutes an emergency
- Automatic retroactive review
- Safeguards against abuse

---

## Key Takeaways

- Fee distribution (20-40% of revenue to stakers) is the cleanest value accrual mechanism
- Buyback-burn creates value for all holders through supply reduction, executed via TWAP
- Ve-models incentivize long-term commitment but face the liquid wrapper problem
- Staking mechanisms provide economic security with slashing deterrence
- One-token-one-vote with delegation is the standard governance model
- Quorum should scale with proposal importance (2-10% standard, 10-20% critical)
- Emergency mechanisms (guardian multi-sig, fast-track) are essential for crisis response
- All emergency powers must be subject to retroactive governance ratification
