# 12.1 Token Supply and Allocation Design

Token supply design and allocation are the foundational decisions of tokenomics. Supply determines scarcity dynamics; allocation determines who benefits from the protocol's success and who has governance power.

{% hint style="info" %}
**Learning Objectives**

After completing this section, you will be able to:
- Compare fixed, inflationary, deflationary, and dynamic supply models
- Select appropriate genesis supply and emission schedule
- Design token allocation across four stakeholder categories within industry benchmarks
- Analyze the insider allocation tradeoffs and apply lessons from major TGEs
{% endhint %}

---

## 12.1.1 Token Supply Models

### Fixed Supply

Total tokens set at genesis; no new tokens can ever be minted.

| | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-|-----------|--------------|
| | Simplicity and clarity | Cannot fund future incentives without selling treasury |
| | No dilution to holders | Limited operational flexibility |
| | Scarcity supports value | If treasury depletes, no recovery mechanism |

**Best for:** Mature protocols with established revenue that don't need inflationary incentives.
**Examples:** UNI (1B fixed supply, though governance can authorize inflation after 4 years)

### Inflationary Supply

New tokens continuously minted on a defined schedule.

| | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-|-----------|--------------|
| | Perpetual source for rewards, compensation, incentives | Dilutes existing holders |
| | Funds operations without depleting treasury | Creates selling pressure from emission recipients |

**Best for:** L1/L2 chains (validator rewards), growth-stage protocols needing aggressive user acquisition.

{% hint style="warning" %}
**Sustainable Inflation Rates**

- **Mature protocols:** 1-5% annually
- **Growth-stage protocols:** Up to 10-15%, with clear declining schedule
- **Above 15%:** Generally unsustainable — signals poor tokenomic design
{% endhint %}

**Examples:** ETH (~0.5-1% issuance, partially offset by burns), MATIC/POL

### Deflationary Supply

Tokens periodically removed from circulation through burns.

| | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-|-----------|--------------|
| | Positive price pressure via decreasing supply | Can create hoarding (the "gold bug" problem) |
| | Aligns value with usage (more usage = more burns) | Inflexible — cannot reverse burns |

**Best for:** Protocols with sustainable revenue exceeding operational needs.
**Examples:** MKR/SKY (surplus auction burns), BNB (quarterly burns), ETH (net deflationary during high activity)

### Dynamic Supply (Recommended)

Moderately inflationary with revenue-funded burns, converging toward zero or deflationary over time.

```
Dynamic Supply = Moderate Inflation (2-8%, declining over 4-8 years)
               + Revenue-Funded Burns
               → Terminal Rate: 0-2% inflation (or net deflationary)
```

{% hint style="success" %}
**Why Dynamic Is Best**

This model provides operational flexibility during growth while creating a long-term path to deflationary economics as the protocol matures. The investment banker should model expected net supply change (inflation minus burns) under different revenue scenarios.
{% endhint %}

## 12.1.2 Genesis Supply

The nominal number of tokens at genesis is economically meaningless — markets value total supply in dollar terms. But practical considerations matter:

- **Convention:** 100 million to 10 billion tokens is standard
- **Recommendation:** 1 billion tokens — accessible unit price, avoids precision issues
- **Psychological anchoring:** A $1 token "feels" more accessible than a $1,000 token, even at the same market cap

## 12.1.3 Emission Schedule Patterns

| Pattern | Description | Best For |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Linear** | Constant tokens per period | Simplicity; lacks sophistication |
| **Declining (Halving)** | Emission cut 50% at regular intervals | Predictable scarcity events (Bitcoin model) |
| **Smooth Exponential Decay** | Continuous rate decrease | Avoids halving supply shocks |
| **Demand-Responsive** | Adjusts based on protocol metrics | Most sophisticated; requires careful anti-manipulation design |
| **Governance-Controlled** | Rate set by governance votes | Maximum flexibility; maximum uncertainty |

**Recommendation:** Declining emission with governance override within defined bounds (e.g., ±25% of base schedule). Coded base schedule provides predictability; governance override provides flexibility.

## 12.2 Token Allocation Design

### 12.2.1 Stakeholder Categories

#### Core Team and Contributors (15-25%)

Primary compensation mechanism. Must attract and retain talent without creating perception of insider extraction.

**Vesting standard:** 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff. No tokens vest in Year 1; 25% at the 1-year mark; remaining 75% monthly or quarterly over 3 years. Some protocols add a 6-12 month lock-up before vesting begins (effective 5-year timeline).

#### Investors (10-20%)

Early-stage investors who funded development. Allocation reflects capital contributed, stage risk, and non-monetary value (strategic guidance, network, technical support).

**Vesting standard:** 2-4 years with 6-12 month cliff, beginning at TGE. Recent launches trending toward longer lock-ups (up to 18 months before vesting starts).

#### Community / Ecosystem (25-45%)

The broadest category: airdrops (5-15%), liquidity mining/rewards (10-25%), and ecosystem grants (5-15%).

{% hint style="success" %}
**Regulatory Significance**

Broad community distribution is critical for both governance decentralization and regulatory positioning. A broadly distributed governance token with no expectation of profit from the efforts of others is less likely to be classified as a security.
{% endhint %}

#### DAO Treasury / Protocol Reserve (20-35%)

Tokens retained for future strategic use — investments, hiring, partnerships, unforeseen needs. Below 20% limits flexibility; above 35% concentrates too many tokens under governance control.

### 12.2.2 The Insider Allocation Debate

Combined team + investor allocation is the most scrutinized TGE metric.

**Historical evolution:**

| Era | Typical Insider % | Community Response |
|-----|------------------|-------------------|
| 2017-2018 (ICO) | 40-60% | Tolerated, then rejected |
| 2019-2021 | 30-45% | "Fair launch" movement pushed for lower |
| 2022-Present | **25-40%** | Industry standard with strong vesting |

Above 40% faces community resistance. Below 20% raises questions about team alignment.

### 12.2.3 Comparative TGE Allocations

| Metric | Uniswap (2020) | Optimism (2022) | Arbitrum (2023) | Jito (2023) |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Team** | 21.5% | 19% | 27% | 24.5% |
| **Investors** | 18% | 17% | 17.5% | 16.2% |
| **Community** | 43% | 44% (incl. future airdrops) | 11.6% (airdrop) | 34.3% |
| **Treasury/Ecosystem** | 17.5% | — (ecosystem 25%, RetroPGF 20%) | 42.8% | 25% |
| **Total Insider** | 39.5% | 36% | 44.5% | 40.7% |
| **Vesting** | 4-year continuous | 4-year, 1-year cliff | 4-year, starts 1 yr post-TGE | Standard |

{% hint style="info" %}
**Key Takeaways from Comparative Analysis**

- **Optimal insider allocation:** 35-40%
- **Optimal community distribution:** 35-45% via airdrops + incentives
- **Optimal treasury:** 20-35%
- **Vesting standard:** 4 years with 1-year cliff
- Uniswap's retroactive airdrop created enormous goodwill and set the industry standard
- Optimism's multi-airdrop strategy (reserving 14% for future drops) incentivized sustained engagement
- Arbitrum's 44.5% insider allocation was at the edge of community tolerance
{% endhint %}

---

## Key Takeaways

- Dynamic supply (moderate inflation + revenue-funded burns) is the recommended model for most protocols
- 1 billion genesis tokens is the standard starting point
- Declining emission with governance override provides predictability with flexibility
- Team (15-25%) + Investors (10-20%) + Community (25-45%) + Treasury (20-35%) is the allocation framework
- Combined insider allocation should target 35-40% with strong vesting (4 years, 1-year cliff)
- Multi-airdrop strategies incentivize sustained engagement beyond initial claim
